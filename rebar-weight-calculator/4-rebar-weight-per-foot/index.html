Worked example

You need 50 #4 bars, each 20 ft long. One bar weighs 20 × 0.668, or 13.36 lb. 50 bars weigh 668.00 lb. That is 0.334 short tons, or 303.00 kg. With 5 percent added for laps and cut-offs the order weight is 701.40 lb.

Where this bar is used

The #4 bar, at 1/2 inch, is one of the most common sizes on houses and small commercial work. Slabs on grade, driveways, footings, masonry walls and small retaining walls often call for it. The plans set the spacing.

Because #4 is so common, yards stock it in many lengths and often sell it by the bundle. Ask the yard how many pieces are in a bundle before you convert a count to tons.

#4 compared with nearby sizes

A #4 bar weighs 78 percent more per foot than a #3 bar (0.376 lb/ft). A #5 bar weighs 56 percent more than a #4 bar (1.043 lb/ft). Moving one size up adds weight fast, because weight follows the area of the bar. Check the plans before you swap one size for another.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 20 ft #4 bar weigh?

A 20 ft #4 bar weighs 13.36 lb, or 6.06 kg. A 40 ft bar weighs 26.72 lb and a 60 ft bar weighs 40.08 lb.

How many #4 bars make a ton?

About 150 bars of 20 ft make one short ton (2,000 lb). With 40 ft bars the count is about 75.

What is the diameter of #4 rebar?

The nominal diameter is 0.5 inch (12.7 mm). The bar number is the diameter in eighths of an inch. The cross-section area is 0.20 square inches.

What is #4 rebar used for?

The #4 bar is used in slabs on grade, driveways, footings, masonry walls and stirrups. It is 1/2 inch in diameter. In ASTM A615M it is called metric #13.
