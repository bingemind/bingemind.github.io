Worked example

You need 50 #8 bars, each 20 ft long. One bar weighs 20 × 2.670, or 53.40 lb. 50 bars weigh 2,670.00 lb. That is 1.335 short tons, or 1,211.09 kg. With 5 percent added for laps and cut-offs the order weight is 2,803.50 lb.

Where this bar is used

The #8 bar is exactly 1 inch in nominal diameter. It carries large loads in columns, pier caps, heavy beams and bridge parts.

A 20 ft piece weighs 53.4 lb and a 60 ft piece weighs 160.2 lb. Plan the lifting before the bars arrive. Long pieces need two people, a lift or a crane.

#8 compared with nearby sizes

A #8 bar weighs 31 percent more per foot than a #7 bar (2.044 lb/ft). A #9 bar weighs 27 percent more than a #8 bar (3.400 lb/ft). Moving one size up adds weight fast, because weight follows the area of the bar. Check the plans before you swap one size for another.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 20 ft #8 bar weigh?

A 20 ft #8 bar weighs 53.40 lb, or 24.22 kg. A 40 ft bar weighs 106.80 lb and a 60 ft bar weighs 160.20 lb.

How many #8 bars make a ton?

About 37 bars of 20 ft make one short ton (2,000 lb). With 40 ft bars the count is about 19.

What is the diameter of #8 rebar?

The nominal diameter is 1 inch (25.4 mm). The bar number is the diameter in eighths of an inch. The cross-section area is 0.79 square inches.

What is #8 rebar used for?

The #8 bar is used in columns, pier caps, heavy beams and bridge elements. It is 1 inch in diameter. Its metric name is M25.
