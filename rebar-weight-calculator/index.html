What a rebar weight calculator does

A rebar weight calculator turns a bar size, a length and a count into a weight. You need that number to price steel, plan a delivery, check a truck load or compare a supplier quote with your takeoff. This calculator uses the nominal weights from ASTM A615, the standard for deformed carbon steel bars in the United States. It gives the total in pounds, kilograms, short tons and metric tons at the same time.

How to calculate rebar weight

  1. Pick the bar size. In the US the number is the diameter in eighths of an inch. A #5 bar is 5/8 inch.
  2. Enter the length of one bar in feet and inches.
  3. Enter how many bars have that length.
  4. Add a line for each other size or length. The totals update as you type.
  5. Add a waste percentage if you want an allowance for laps and cut-offs.

The math is short. Weight equals pounds per foot times length times quantity. For #3 to #8 bars you can also use a shortcut: the bar number squared times 0.04172 gives pounds per foot. For a #6 bar that is 36 × 0.04172, or 1.502 lb/ft.

Worked example

You need 100 pieces of #5 bar, each 20 feet long. A #5 bar weighs 1.043 lb/ft. Each piece weighs 20 × 1.043, or 20.86 lb. One hundred pieces weigh 2,086 lb. That is 1.043 short tons, or about 946 kg. Add 5 percent for laps and cut-offs and the order weight becomes 2,190 lb.

Rebar weight per foot and per meter

The weight per foot comes from the cross-section area of the bar and the density of steel, about 490 lb per cubic foot. The nominal diameter is a round number. The real shape has ribs, so the standard lists a nominal weight for each size. Use that number, not a value from the diameter alone.

The metric chart lists two groups. The first group holds the ASTM metric designations, such as M16, which is the same steel as a US #5. The second group holds plain round diameters in millimeters. Their weight in kg/m is the diameter squared divided by 162.2. A 12 mm bar weighs 144 / 162.2, or 0.888 kg/m.

Steel density also lets you check a weight by hand. A bar with a 1 inch diameter has an area of 0.785 square inches. Multiply by 12 inches and by 0.2836 pounds per cubic inch and you get 2.67 pounds per foot, the value for a #8 bar.

Tips for a reliable takeoff

  • Count laps. A splice usually adds 30 to 40 bar diameters. Add them to the length, or use the waste field.
  • Bends add length. Measure bent bars along the bar, not in a straight line between the ends.
  • Allow for cut-offs. Bars arrive in stock lengths, and the remainder is often scrap.
  • Buy in stock lengths. The Bars to order box divides your total length by 20, 40 or 60 ft. It does not plan the cuts, so ask the yard if they can cut to size.
  • Check the units on a quote. A short ton is 2,000 lb. A metric ton is 1,000 kg, or 2,204.6 lb.
  • Expect small differences on the scale. ASTM A615 lets an individual bar weigh up to 6 percent under the nominal weight.

Which bar sizes are common

Small sizes do most of the work in slabs and walls. #3 and #4 bars appear in residential slabs, footings and stirrups. #5 and #6 bars carry beams, columns and foundation walls. #7 to #11 bars show up in bridge decks, piers and heavy footings. The #14 and #18 sizes are rare. Mills often roll them to order, so check lead times before you design around them.

On a job with many sizes, group the bars by size first. Enter one line per size and length, then read the weight on each line. This also makes it easy to compare your takeoff with the supplier's bar list, which is usually grouped the same way.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate the weight of rebar?

Multiply the weight per foot by the length and by the number of bars. A #5 bar weighs 1.043 lb/ft. One 20 foot bar weighs 20.86 lb, and 100 bars weigh 2,086 lb. This calculator does the steps for every line you enter and adds them up.

How much does #4 rebar weigh per foot?

A #4 bar weighs 0.668 lb per foot, which is 0.994 kg per meter. A 20 foot bar weighs 13.36 lb. About 150 bars of 20 feet make one short ton.

How many pieces of rebar are in a ton?

Divide 2,000 lb by the weight of one bar. For 20 foot bars, a ton holds about 266 pieces of #3, 150 of #4, 96 of #5, 67 of #6 and 49 of #7. The chart on this page lists the feet per ton for every size.

What does #5 rebar weigh?

A #5 bar weighs 1.043 lb per foot and has a nominal diameter of 0.625 inch. A 20 foot length weighs 20.86 lb and a 40 foot length weighs 41.72 lb.

What do the rebar numbers mean?

For bars #3 to #8 the number is the diameter in eighths of an inch. A #6 bar is 6/8 inch, or 0.75 inch. Sizes #9, #10, #11, #14 and #18 are set by area. Their diameters match the old square bar sizes.

Are metric rebar sizes the same bars?

The ASTM metric sizes are the same steel with new names. An M13 bar is a #4 bar, and an M16 bar is a #5 bar. Switch the calculator to Metric to enter lengths in meters and read kilograms first. It also lists plain round diameters from 6 to 40 mm.

Should I add waste to the rebar weight?

Most estimators add an allowance for laps, hooks and cut-offs. The size depends on the job, so the calculator starts at 0 percent. Type a number in the waste field and the total updates. The net weight stays visible below the total.

How exact is the result?

It uses the nominal weights in ASTM A615 and A615M. Real bars vary a little. The standard allows an individual bar to weigh up to 6 percent under nominal. For large orders, ask the supplier for mill weights or scale tickets.
