    How take-home pay is calculated

    Your take-home pay (net pay) is your gross pay minus taxes and deductions. The calculator works out your tax for the whole year, then divides it by your number of paychecks, which is how a correctly filled-in W-4 spreads withholding.

    Take-home pay = gross pay
     − federal income tax
     − Social Security (6.2% up to $184,500)
     − Medicare (1.45%, plus 0.9% over $200,000)
     − state and local income tax
     − pre-tax deductions (401(k), health, HSA)

    How to increase your take-home pay and read your pay stub

    Gross pay, net pay and the lines in between

    Your pay stub starts with gross pay and ends with net pay. In between are taxes that are required, such as federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare in the US, and deductions that you choose, such as a 401(k) or health insurance. The chart in the results shows how each part takes a share of every paycheck.

    What changes the result

    • Filing status: married filing jointly has a larger standard deduction and wider brackets than single filing, so tax is lower on the same income.
    • Pay frequency: a salary paid weekly or monthly gives the same yearly total. Only the size of each paycheck changes.
    • State: nine states have no tax on wages, and other states charge a flat rate or progressive rates.
    • Pre-tax deductions: 401(k), health premiums and an HSA reduce income tax. Social Security and Medicare still apply to most of them.
    • Bonuses: federal withholding on a bonus is often a flat 22% when it is paid separately, which can look high, but the real tax is settled when you file your return.

    Ways to keep more of your pay

    Contribute to a traditional 401(k), especially up to your employer match, since the match is extra pay. Use an HSA if you have a qualifying health plan, because contributions, growth and medical withdrawals can all be tax-free. Review your W-4 after a marriage, a new child or a second job. If you get a large refund each year, you are lending the government money, and adjusting your W-4 raises your paycheck now. If you owe at filing time, you may be under-withholding. Try different values in the calculator to see the effect before you change anything.

    Hourly workers and overtime

    Choose hourly pay and enter your hours per week. The calculator turns it into a yearly figure assuming you work 52 weeks. Overtime, tips and shift differentials are not part of that estimate, so add them to the hourly rate or use the overtime calculator and then enter the weekly total.

    Reading the effective and marginal rates

    The effective tax rate is total tax divided by gross pay. The marginal rate is the tax on your next dollar of income. A raise never lowers your take-home pay in the US, because only the dollars above a bracket threshold are taxed at the higher rate. That is the reason a promotion that moves you into the next bracket still leaves you with more money.

    What this estimate leaves out

    Local and city taxes, state disability programs in some states, wage garnishments, union dues, retirement plans other than a 401(k), student loan payments and itemised deductions are not included. Outside the US, the calculator covers the main income tax and social contributions for each country listed, but not every regional tax, benefit or allowance. Use it to plan and compare, and rely on your pay stub or a tax professional for the exact figure.

    Social Security and Medicare in the US

    Employees pay Social Security at 6.2% on wages up to the yearly wage base ($184,500 in 2026) and Medicare at 1.45% on all wages, plus 0.9% on wages above $200,000. Your employer pays a matching amount on top. Self-employed people pay both halves, which is 15.3% on most of their earnings, so this calculator is for employees and not for freelancers.

    State income tax

    Nine states do not tax wages. A few states use a flat rate, and the rest use brackets. Some cities, such as New York City, add a local income tax. Where a state is not listed, choose Other and enter the state rate you want to test. The calculator treats state income tax as a percentage of taxable pay and does not include local taxes, so the result for those places is a little high.

    Comparing two job offers

    Compare take-home pay and not just salary. A job in a state with no income tax can pay more after tax than a higher salary in a state with a 6% to 9% income tax. Also count what the employer pays for, such as health insurance, a 401(k) match and paid leave, since these have real money value even though they do not appear in your paycheck. Run each offer through the calculator with the same filing status, then compare the yearly net figures.

    If you work in a different country

    Pick your country at the top of the page. The calculator switches the currency, number format and tax rules. Countries without a built-in tax model use the rates you enter, so you can still get an estimate from a payslip. Tax residency, treaties and expatriate rules are complicated, so speak to a tax adviser if you work across borders.

    Understanding withholding, refunds and estimated tax

    Withholding and your tax bill are different things

    Withholding is the amount your employer sends to the tax authority from each paycheck. Your tax bill is what you owe for the year. If you withhold more than you owe, you get a refund. If you withhold less, you owe the difference. The calculator shows the tax for the year spread across paychecks, which is close to what correct withholding would be.

    When to adjust your W-4

    Review it when you marry, divorce, have a child, take a second job, or when your spouse starts or stops working. A second job is a common reason for under-withholding, since each employer assumes it is your only income. The IRS has a free Tax Withholding Estimator for a precise answer.

    Side income and estimated tax

    Income that has no tax withheld, such as freelance work, may require quarterly estimated tax payments in the US. A common safe-harbour rule is to pay at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax, with a higher percentage for high earners, to avoid an underpayment penalty. Use a separate calculation for self-employment tax.

    Pay raises and the bracket myth

    People worry that a raise will move them into a higher bracket and reduce their pay. It will not. Only the part of income above the threshold is taxed at the higher rate, so more gross pay always gives more take-home pay. Try it by changing the salary in the calculator by a few thousand dollars.

    Year-end bonuses and stock

    Bonuses and vesting stock are taxed as ordinary income, and employers often withhold a flat 22% federal rate on supplemental pay under a set amount. Your actual tax depends on your total income, so you may owe more or get some back at filing time. Add the bonus to your salary in the calculator for a yearly estimate.

    Currency and rounding

    Amounts are shown in your local currency and rounded to the nearest cent, or to the whole unit for yearly totals. Small differences from your pay stub are normal because payroll systems round each line.

    Take-home pay questions

    How much is a 40,000 pound salary after tax in the UK?

    For 2026/27 in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, a salary of £40,000 is about £32,320 a year after income tax and National Insurance, or about £2,693 a month. That is an effective tax rate of about 19%. A workplace pension lowers the income tax part.

    How much is 90,000 Australian dollars after tax?

    For a resident in 2026-27, A$90,000 pays about A$17,520 income tax and A$1,800 Medicare levy, leaving about A$70,680 a year, or A$5,890 a month. Your employer pays super on top, so it is not deducted.

    What is take-home pay on 70,000 Canadian dollars in Ontario?

    On C$70,000 in Ontario for 2026, take-home pay is about C$53,800 a year after federal and Ontario income tax, CPP and EI. That is about C$4,480 a month. Other provinces differ, and Quebec has its own pension and tax system.

    How is tax calculated in India under the new regime?

    For FY 2026-27 the new regime gives a 75,000 rupee standard deduction, no tax up to 4 lakh, then 5% to 30% slabs, and a rebate that makes income up to 12 lakh taxable tax-free. Add 4% cess. A salary of 15 lakh pays about 97,500 rupees tax and keeps about 14.03 lakh.

    Does this work for other countries?

    Pick your country in the header. The UK, Canada, Australia, India and Gulf states have their own tax rules built in. For any other country, enter your average income tax and social security rates from a payslip, and the calculator shows your take-home pay in your currency.

    How much is $60,000 a year after taxes?

    For a single filer in a state with no income tax, $60,000 a year is about $50,390 after federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare in 2026. That is about $1,938 every two weeks or $4,199 a month. State income tax and a 401(k) lower it further.

    What percentage of my paycheck goes to taxes?

    Most people pay between 15% and 30% of their pay in total taxes. FICA is a flat 7.65% for most workers, and federal income tax depends on your income and filing status. State income tax adds 0% to over 10% depending on where you live.

    What is the difference between gross pay and net pay?

    Gross pay is what you earn before anything is taken out. Net pay, or take-home pay, is what is left after taxes and deductions such as 401(k) contributions and health insurance premiums.

    Does a 401(k) contribution lower my taxes?

    A traditional 401(k) contribution lowers your federal income tax and, in most states, your state income tax, because it comes out before tax. It does not lower Social Security or Medicare tax. In 2026 you can contribute up to $24,500. Roth 401(k) contributions do not lower your tax now.

    Which states have no income tax?

    Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming have no state income tax on wages. Washington does tax some capital gains, and some cities in other states charge local income tax.

    Why is my paycheck different from this estimate?

    Your employer withholds based on your W-4, and it may withhold more or less than your final tax. Local taxes, state disability or family leave programs, bonuses, overtime and other deductions also change a real paycheck. Use this as an estimate and compare it with your pay stub.

    How is the child tax credit included?

    For 2026 the child tax credit is up to $2,200 per child under 17. It reduces your federal income tax and starts to phase out above $200,000 of income ($400,000 married filing jointly). This calculator applies it to your yearly tax, as your W-4 would if you claim it there.
