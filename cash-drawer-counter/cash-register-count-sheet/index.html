How to fill out the count sheet

  1. Write the date, your name, the register number and the shift at the top.
  2. At the start of the shift, count the drawer. Write the count of each bill and coin in the Opening column. Write the amount next to it. Add the Opening amounts at the bottom.
  3. At the end of the shift, count again and fill in the Closing columns the same way.
  4. Write the starting float. Write the cash sales from the register report and any paid-outs.
  5. Add the float and the cash sales, then subtract the paid-outs. That is the expected cash in the drawer.
  6. Subtract the expected cash from the closing total. A plus number is over. A minus number is short.
  7. Subtract the float from the closing total to get the deposit. Sign the sheet.

Opening and closing columns

The Opening column shows what was in the drawer when the shift began. The Closing column shows what is in it now. The opening total should equal the float. If it does not, write the difference at the top so the closing check stays fair for the next shift.

Why use a count sheet

A written count gives you proof of what was in the drawer and who counted it. If the drawer is short, the sheet shows where to look. Many shops keep the sheet with the deposit slip and the register report.

Skip the math

The sums on this sheet take time and add chances for mistakes. The cash drawer counter does them for you. It also lists the bills to leave and the bills to take out for the deposit, and it prints a filled sheet.
