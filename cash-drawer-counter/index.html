      How to count a cash drawer (fast)
       
      The cash drawer counter on this page adds up the bills and coins in your register at the end of a shift. You type how many of each you have. The tool does the math, shows the total, and tells you how much to deposit. It also shows what to leave in the drawer and which bills and coins to take out.
       
      Count the drawer in six steps
       
      Want the quickest method? Read the guide on the fastest way to count a cash drawer.
       
         
      1. Close the sale screen so no new sales come in while you count.
      2. Take all cash out of the drawer, including the bills under the tray.
      3. Sort it by type. Face all bills the same way and put the largest on top.
      4. Count each type once and enter the number in its row. Start with the $100 bills and work down to the pennies.
      5. Enter your starting float. The tool shows the cash to deposit, what to leave in the drawer, and what to take out.
      6. Enter the expected cash sales from your register report. The tool shows if you are over or short.
        7.  
       
      Press Enter after each row to jump to the next one. On a phone, the number keypad opens by itself. This is the same count that shops call a till count, a register balance or "cashing up".
       
      What is a float?
       
      A float is the cash you leave in the drawer so the next shift can make change. It is also called a bank, a till float, or a starting bank. Many shops use $100 to $300. The float is not sales money. You count it, then you leave it.
       
      Your deposit is the total in the drawer minus the float. If you count $1,250.00 and the float is $200.00, you deposit $1,050.00.
       
      How the take-out list works
       
      The tool picks the float first, so the next shift can make change. It aims for a mix: about 40% in $20 bills, then $10 bills, $5 bills and $1 bills, with a few quarters, dimes, nickels and pennies. If the drawer lacks one kind, it fills the gap with what you have, largest first. It never leaves $100 or $50 bills in the drawer. Everything else goes in the deposit. The tool shows a green check when the bills it leaves add up to exactly your float.
      If the cash can make the float exactly, the tool finds a way to do it. If it cannot, the tool says how much is missing, so you can swap bills by hand. The deposit shown is always the value of the take-out list.
       
      Tips to avoid counting errors
       
         
      • Count twice if the drawer is off by more than a few dollars. Count a different way the second time, for example by stack size.
        •  
      • Count coins in rolls or stacks of ten. Do not count them one by one.
        •  
      • Check for stuck bills. Two bills can stick together, especially when new.
        •  
      • Do not mix checks, gift cards or coupons with cash. Count them apart.
        •  
      • Enter the expected sales from the same time you stopped sales. A late sale causes a false shortage.
        •  
      • Have a second person watch or sign the count if your shop has a rule for it.
        •

      Over and short
       
      Over means you have more cash than the register says. Short means less. A small difference is common, often from wrong change. A large or repeated difference needs a check of voids, refunds, tips, and paid-outs. The tool works out the cash the drawer should hold: the float plus cash sales, minus any paid-outs. It compares that with your count. Use cash sales only, not card sales. There is a worked example on the over and short calculator page.
       
      How to close out a register
       
      To close out a register, or cash out a till, stop sales and print the end-of-shift report. Take the till out and count it once with this calculator. Enter the cash sales from the report. Leave the float in the drawer, put the deposit in a bag with the count sheet, and sign it. A cash drop during the shift works the same way: count what you take out and write it down. The cash drop calculator page shows how.
       
      Who uses a drawer count
       
      Cashiers count at the end of every shift. Shift leads count when one cashier hands the drawer to the next. Owners of small shops and food trucks count to match the cash against the sales report before the bank run. The steps are the same for each of them, so one calculator fits all three.

      Half dollars
       
      Half dollars are under More coins, below the rolls. Most drawers have none, so the row stays closed until you open it.

      Frequently asked questions
       
       
        Is this an online cash drawer counter?   
      Yes. It runs in your web browser, so there is nothing to download or sign up for. Open the page, enter your bills and coins, and read the total, the deposit and the take-out list.
       
        Is there a cash drawer counter app?   
      There is no app to install. This is a web app that opens in the browser on your phone, tablet or computer. It saves your last float on your device, and it works the same on each of them.
       
        How do I count a cash drawer?   
      Take out all cash, sort it by bill and coin type, and count each type once. Enter the counts in the calculator. It adds up each row and the grand total. Then subtract your float to get the cash to deposit.
       
        What is a cash drawer float?   
      A float is the cash you leave in the register for the next shift to make change. It is also called a starting bank or a till float. You count it, but it is not part of the deposit.
       
        How much should I leave in the drawer?   
      It depends on your shop. Many small shops leave $100 to $300 in a mix of small bills and coins. Use the amount your manager or owner sets.
       
        How do I know if the drawer is over or short?   
      Enter the expected cash sales from your register report. Use cash only, not card sales. The calculator adds the float to the cash sales, subtracts any paid-outs you enter, and compares the result with your count. Green means over or balanced. Red means short.
       
        What are coin rolls worth?   
      A roll of quarters is $10, dimes $5, nickels $2, and pennies $0.50. Enter full rolls in the roll rows, and loose coins in the coin rows.
       
        Does it work on my phone?   
      Yes. The number keypad opens on each field and the buttons are large, so you can count with one hand. Dark mode follows your phone setting.
       
        Is my count saved or sent anywhere?   
      No. The tool runs in your browser and sends nothing to a server. Your last float and your current counts are saved on your own device, so you do not retype the float and a refresh does not lose a count. Clear your browser data to remove them, or use Reset to clear the counts.
       
        Can I print or share the count?   
      Yes. Use Print for a clean count sheet with a line to sign, or Copy summary to paste the counts, totals, leave list and take-out list into an email or a message.
       
        What is a cash register count sheet?   
      It is a form that lists each bill and coin, with the count and amount for the start and end of a shift. This site has a free printable blank sheet, and the calculator prints a filled one.
