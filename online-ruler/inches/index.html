    An inch ruler with 1/16 marks

    This page opens the online ruler in inches. Each long mark with a number is a whole inch. The mark in the middle is 1/2 inch and carries a small "½" label. Shorter marks show 1/4, 1/8 and 1/16.

    Pull a guide out of the ruler to mark the end of your object. The tool rounds the length to the nearest 1/16 and shows it as a reduced fraction, such as "3 5/8 in". It also shows the decimal value (3.625 in) and the millimeter value, so you can copy the form you need.

    Calibrate first. A card is 3 3/8 in wide and 2 1/8 in tall, which is a good check on the ruler once you have calibrated it.

    How to read inch fractions

    Count the small marks after the last whole inch. There are 16 steps in an inch. Reduce the fraction by dividing both numbers by 2 until you cannot go further:

    • 8 steps is 8/16, which is 1/2.
    • 4 steps is 4/16, which is 1/4. 12 steps is 3/4.
    • 2 steps is 2/16, which is 1/8. 6 steps is 3/8.
    • Odd counts, such as 1, 3 or 11, stay in sixteenths: 11/16.

    Add the whole inches in front. If the last whole inch is 3 and you count 10 steps, the length is 3 and 10/16, which is 3 5/8 in.

    Inches in everyday US sizes

    Many US sizes use inches: lumber and hardware, paper, frames, screens and clothing. A US Letter sheet is 8.5 by 11 in. A credit card is 3 3/8 by 2 1/8 in. A US quarter is 24.26 mm across, which is about 0.955 in (the US Mint lists 0.955 in).

    When a plan lists a size as a decimal, such as 2.375 in, use the table to find the nearest 1/16. 2.375 in is 2 3/8 in. If you work in metric, the cm ruler uses the same calibration.

    Tips for accurate readings

    • Look straight down at the edge you read. A sideways view shifts the mark.
    • Keep the object flat against the glass.
    • Turn snap on to make guides jump to the nearest 1/16.
    • Use full screen (F) to give the ruler the whole display.
    • Check the result with a coin after you calibrate. If it does not fit, calibrate again.

    Inch fractions at a glance

    The ruler marks every 1/16 inch. The mark length tells you the fraction. The longest mark between two numbers is 1/2. The next length is 1/4 and 3/4. Then 1/8, 3/8, 5/8 and 7/8. The shortest marks are the odd sixteenths.

    A quick way to read a fraction: count the marks past the last whole inch, and write the count over 16. If the count is even, halve both numbers until the count is odd. Six marks past 2 inches is 6/16, which is 3/8, so the length is 2 3/8 in.

    Many tape measures stop at 1/16, and so does this tool. If a plan asks for 1/32, use the decimal value from the readout and convert it with the chart on this page.

    Measuring common household things

    • Picture frames and prints. Standard print sizes are whole inches, such as 4 x 6 and 5 x 7. Check that your print fits the mat opening, which is usually a little smaller.
    • Hardware. Bolts, nuts and washers use fractions of an inch. A 1/4 inch bolt has a shaft 6.35 mm wide. Lay it on the screen and read the width with two guides.
    • Lumber. A board sold as 2 x 4 is smaller than its name. The finished size in the US is 1 1/2 by 3 1/2 inches. Measure the actual piece when it matters.
    • Crafts. Quilt squares, beads and ribbon are often given in inches and sixteenths.

    Inches and millimeters together

    Some jobs use both units. A part may be drawn in inches but sold in millimeters. Choose the "cm + in" view on the main ruler to see both scales along the same edge. The readout also gives the millimeter value for every guide, so you can copy the unit the other person needs.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I read inch fractions: 1/16, 1/8 and 1/4?

    Between two whole inches there are 16 small marks. The longest mark is 1/2. The next longest are 1/4 and 3/4. Then 1/8, 3/8, 5/8 and 7/8. Count the small marks from the last whole inch. Eight marks is 1/2, and 12 marks is 3/4.

    How do I find 1 inch on my screen?

    Calibrate first. Then pick the inch unit. The long mark with the number 1 is one inch from the corner. A US quarter is 24.26 mm wide, which is a little less than one inch (25.4 mm), so it fits inside that span with a gap.

    Can I use a 12 inch online ruler?

    Twelve inches is 304.8 mm. The ruler can only be as long as your screen. A wide desktop monitor can show 12 inches. Most phones cannot, and a small laptop may not. Calibrate first, then check how far the scale runs.

    How do I change inches to cm on the online ruler?

    One inch is exactly 2.54 cm. Pull a guide out to the end of your object. The tool shows the length in your chosen unit and in the other units too, so an inch reading also appears in cm and mm. You can also divide mm by 25.4 to get inches.

    How accurate is an online ruler?

    It depends on the calibration. Before you calibrate, the ruler is a guess based on your screen type, and the size can be well off. After you match a credit card to the outline, the result depends on how closely you line up the edges. Zoom in, use the 0.1 mm buttons, and run the check step with a coin.

    How do I calibrate an online ruler with a credit card?

    Press Calibrate at the top right of the page (on a phone, above the ruler), then choose the Card tab. Hold a card flat against the screen and line up one edge with the outline. Drag the grip on the right edge until the other edge matches. Use the 0.1 mm buttons for fine changes, then press "My card matches". Then check the result with a coin.

    Why does the ruler look too big or too small?

    Most rulers assume 96 pixels per inch. Many screens use a different density, and browsers and operating systems scale the page. The result is a ruler that is too long or too short. Calibrate once and the ruler uses the real size of a pixel on your screen.
