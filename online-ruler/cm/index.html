    A centimeter ruler for your screen

    This page opens the online ruler in centimeters. Each numbered mark is 1 cm. Each small mark is 1 mm, and the middle mark between two numbers is 5 mm. The zero mark sits in the top left corner.

    To measure, put one end of the object at zero. Then pull a guide out of the ruler and move it to the other end. The tool shows the length, for example "9.21 cm = 92.1 mm = 3 5/8 in". Press Copy to put the text on your clipboard.

    For a true size ruler, calibrate first. Press Calibrate at the top right (on a phone, above the ruler), open the Card tab, and match a credit card to the outline. The status then reads "Calibrated".

    Centimeters and millimeters

    The centimeter is a tenth of a decimeter and a hundredth of a meter. There are 10 millimeters in a centimeter. To turn cm into mm, multiply by 10. To turn mm into cm, divide by 10. So 4.7 cm is 47 mm, and 63 mm is 6.3 cm.

    To switch between centimeters and inches, use 2.54: one inch is exactly 2.54 cm. Use the table below for common values. The millimeter ruler shows the same scale with a number every 10 mm, which is easier for small parts.

    What people measure in centimeters

    • Books, notebooks and photo prints.
    • Phone cases, cables and small electronics.
    • Craft pieces, fabric swatches and patterns.
    • Pots, plants and seed spacing, on a flat sheet or card.
    • Items to ship, when you want to check a box size before you buy it.

    If the object is longer than your screen, measure in parts. Mark the end of the first part with a guide, slide the object, and add the lengths. This adds a small error each time, so use a tape measure for anything long.

    Get the most from the cm ruler

    1. Calibrate with a card, and run the check step with a coin.
    2. Press F for full screen, so the rulers use the whole display.
    3. Turn on snap in Options if you want guides to jump to the nearest millimeter.
    4. Use the arrow keys on a selected guide to move it one millimeter at a time. Hold Shift for steps of 10.
    5. Need inches too? Switch to "cm + in" to see both scales on one ruler, or open the inch ruler.

    The ruler works the same on a phone. Turn the phone sideways for more length. See the phone ruler page for tips.

    Reading a centimeter ruler, step by step

    Put the object at zero. Find the last numbered mark before the end of the object. That number is the whole centimeters. Then count the small marks after it. Each small mark is one millimeter.

    Example: an object ends 3 marks after the 7. That is 7 cm and 3 mm, which you write as 7.3 cm. The tool does this for you when you use a guide, but the same steps work when you read the marks by eye.

    Half-centimeter marks help. The middle mark between two numbers is 5 mm, and it is a little longer than the others. If an object ends at that mark, add 0.5 cm to the last whole number.

    Common sizes in centimeters

    These reference sizes are handy for a quick check. A credit card is 8.56 cm by 5.398 cm. A US quarter is 2.426 cm across. A US dime is 1.791 cm across. A sheet of A4 paper is 21.0 cm by 29.7 cm. A US Letter sheet is 21.59 cm by 27.94 cm. All of these come from standards or the US Mint.

    If you have one of these on your desk, lay it on the screen and compare. This is the same idea as the check step, and it takes five seconds.

    When centimeters are the right choice

    Use centimeters when you want a number that is easy to say and to write. 12.4 cm is quicker to communicate than 124 mm, and it avoids the fractions of an inch. Most countries use metric for clothing, furniture and packaging, so centimeters are the usual unit in shops and online listings.

    If a form asks for millimeters, switch to the mm ruler. If a pattern is in inches, switch to the inch ruler. Your calibration stays the same, so you do not need to repeat it.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I measure in cm and mm online?

    Choose cm or mm in the unit switch. Place the object against the ruler from the zero mark at the corner. Each small mark is 1 mm and every tenth mark is 1 cm. Pull a guide out of the ruler to mark the far edge and read the length.

    How do I change inches to cm on the online ruler?

    One inch is exactly 2.54 cm. Pull a guide out to the end of your object. The tool shows the length in your chosen unit and in the other units too, so an inch reading also appears in cm and mm. You can also divide mm by 25.4 to get inches.

    How accurate is an online ruler?

    It depends on the calibration. Before you calibrate, the ruler is a guess based on your screen type, and the size can be well off. After you match a credit card to the outline, the result depends on how closely you line up the edges. Zoom in, use the 0.1 mm buttons, and run the check step with a coin.

    How do I calibrate an online ruler with a credit card?

    Press Calibrate at the top right of the page (on a phone, above the ruler), then choose the Card tab. Hold a card flat against the screen and line up one edge with the outline. Drag the grip on the right edge until the other edge matches. Use the 0.1 mm buttons for fine changes, then press "My card matches". Then check the result with a coin.

    Why does the ruler look too big or too small?

    Most rulers assume 96 pixels per inch. Many screens use a different density, and browsers and operating systems scale the page. The result is a ruler that is too long or too short. Calibrate once and the ruler uses the real size of a pixel on your screen.

    Can I use my phone as a ruler? How long can it measure?

    Yes. Open this page on the phone and calibrate. The ruler can only be as long as the screen. A 6.1 inch phone has a screen about 14 cm tall, so it measures small objects. Turn the phone sideways to use the longer edge.

    Is it free? Do I need to install an app?

    It is free and runs in the browser. There is nothing to install and no account to make.
