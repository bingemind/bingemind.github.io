How the mortgage payment is calculated

Lenders use the standard amortization formula. Each month the same payment covers that month's interest first, and the rest reduces the balance. Early payments are mostly interest; late payments are mostly principal.

Monthly principal and interest = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)
P = loan amount (price − down payment)
r = monthly rate = yearly rate ÷ 12
n = number of monthly payments (years × 12)

Your real monthly cost is more than principal and interest. This calculator adds:

  • Property tax, entered as a yearly percentage of the price and divided by 12. In the US most lenders collect it through an escrow account.
  • Home insurance, the yearly premium divided by 12.
  • Mortgage insurance (PMI) when you put down less than 20% on a US conventional loan. It is charged on the loan amount and, by law, stops automatically when the balance reaches 78% of the original price. The calculator shows when that happens.
  • HOA dues or service charges for condos, townhouses and leasehold flats.

Canadian mortgages compound twice a year

In Canada, fixed-rate mortgages must state interest compounded semi-annually, not in advance. That makes the effective monthly rate slightly lower than the quoted rate divided by 12. Pick Twice a year under "Interest compounds" (it is chosen for you when your country is Canada). A 5% rate then works out to a monthly rate of 0.412%, not 0.417%.

Rules of thumb lenders use

  • US (28/36 rule): housing costs up to 28% of gross monthly income and all debts up to 36%. Many lenders allow more with strong credit.
  • UK: lenders usually cap the loan at about 4.5 times household income and stress-test the payment at a higher rate.
  • Canada: you must qualify at the higher of 5.25% or your rate plus 2% (the minimum qualifying rate), with gross debt service around 39% and total debt service around 44%.
  • Australia: lenders test your payment at your rate plus a 3% serviceability buffer.
  • India: banks usually keep the EMI under 40% to 50% of net monthly income.

Extra payments: the fastest way to save

Because interest is charged on the remaining balance, every extra amount you pay goes straight to principal and cuts all the interest that balance would have charged later. On a 30-year loan the effect is large.

Worked example

A 320,000 loan at 6.3% for 30 years costs 1,980.71 a month in principal and interest and about 393,000 in interest over the full term. Paying 200 extra a month clears it about six and a half years sooner and saves about 100,000 in interest. Enter the same numbers above and set the extra payment to see the exact figures for your loan.

Other ways to pay less interest

  • Biweekly payments: paying half the monthly payment every two weeks adds up to 13 monthly payments a year. Enter one twelfth of your payment as an extra monthly amount to model it.
  • Shorter term: a 15-year loan usually has a lower rate and costs far less interest, but the payment is higher.
  • Bigger down payment: 20% down avoids PMI in the US and gets better rates in most countries.
  • Lump sums: many lenders let you prepay 10% to 20% a year without a penalty. Check your loan terms for early repayment charges first, especially in the UK and Canada.

Mortgage questions

How is a monthly mortgage payment calculated?

Multiply the loan amount by the monthly rate, then divide by 1 minus (1 + monthly rate) to the power of minus the number of payments. A 300,000 loan at 6% for 30 years is 1,798.65 a month in principal and interest. Add property tax, insurance, PMI and HOA dues for the full payment.

What is PMI and when does it stop?

Private mortgage insurance protects a US lender when you put down less than 20%. It usually costs 0.3% to 1.5% of the loan a year. On conventional loans it must stop automatically when your balance is scheduled to reach 78% of the original value, and you can ask to remove it at 80%.

How much do extra payments save on a mortgage?

Extra payments reduce principal directly, so they cut the interest charged on that money for the rest of the loan. On a 320,000, 30-year loan at 6.3%, an extra 200 a month saves about 100,000 in interest and six and a half years.

Why is my Canadian mortgage payment different from a US calculator?

Canadian fixed-rate mortgages compound semi-annually by law, so the monthly rate is (1 + rate ÷ 2) to the power of 1/6, minus 1. That gives a slightly lower payment than dividing the rate by 12. Choose Twice a year under Interest compounds.

Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year mortgage?

A 15-year loan has a higher payment but usually a lower rate and far less total interest. A 30-year loan keeps the payment low and leaves room in your budget. Many people take the 30-year loan and make extra payments when they can.

Does this calculator include closing costs?

No. Closing costs, stamp duty, land transfer tax and legal fees are paid once at purchase. Budget roughly 2% to 6% of the price on top of the down payment, depending on your country.

Is the default interest rate a real quote?

No. The starting rate is a typical figure for your country in late 2026 so the example looks sensible. Replace it with the rate from your lender, because your credit, deposit and loan type change it.
