The compound interest formula

With compound interest, each period's interest is added to the balance, so the next period's interest is calculated on a larger amount.

A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)n × t
A = future value · P = starting amount · r = yearly rate as a decimal · n = times compounded a year · t = years

Example: 10,000 at 7% compounded monthly for 20 years. A = 10,000 × (1 + 0.07 ÷ 12)240 = 40,387. With no extra deposits, the money quadruples.

Adding regular contributions

Future value of deposits = D × ((1 + i)m − 1) ÷ i
D = monthly deposit · i = monthly rate · m = number of months

Adding 500 a month to the same example gives 260,463 from deposits, for a total of 300,851. You put in 130,000 and interest adds 170,851. This calculator runs the sum month by month, so it also handles yearly increases in your deposit and deposits made at the start of the month.

Why starting early matters more than the amount

Time is the strongest input. Compare two savers who both earn 7% a year:

The rule of 72

Divide 72 by the yearly rate to estimate how many years it takes money to double. At 6% it is about 12 years, at 8% about 9 years. The result above shows the exact doubling time for your rate.

The effective yearly rate is called APY in the US and AER in the UK and Ireland. It lets you compare accounts that compound on different schedules.

Inflation, tax and realistic returns

A future balance buys less than the same amount today. The result shows the value in today's money using the inflation rate you enter, which starts at a typical figure for your country.

  • Savings accounts and fixed deposits pay a known rate, often close to inflation.
  • Share market index funds have returned roughly 7% to 10% a year over long periods before inflation, with large swings from year to year. Past returns do not guarantee future ones.
  • Tax reduces growth unless the money is in a tax-sheltered account such as a 401(k) or IRA (US), ISA (UK), TFSA or RRSP (Canada), superannuation (Australia) or PPF and NPS (India).
  • Fees compound too. A 1% yearly fee can take a fifth of the final balance over 30 years. Enter your return after fees.

Compound interest questions

What is compound interest?

It is interest calculated on both the original amount and the interest already added. Because the balance keeps growing, the interest earned each period grows too.

How do I calculate compound interest?

Use A = P × (1 + r ÷ n) to the power of n × t. For 10,000 at 7% compounded monthly for 20 years, the result is about 40,387.

How much will 500 a month be worth in 20 years?

At 7% a year compounded monthly, 500 a month grows to about 260,463 after 20 years. You contribute 120,000 and interest adds about 140,463.

What is the difference between APR and APY?

APR is the stated yearly rate without compounding. APY, called AER in the UK, includes compounding, so it is the rate you actually earn in a year. 6% compounded monthly is an APY of 6.168%.

Does daily compounding make a big difference?

Not much. On 10,000 at 6% for 10 years, daily compounding earns about 26 more than monthly compounding. The rate and the time matter far more.

What is the rule of 72?

A shortcut for doubling time: divide 72 by the yearly interest rate. At 9%, money doubles in about 8 years.
