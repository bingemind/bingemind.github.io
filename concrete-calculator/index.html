How to calculate concrete

Concrete is ordered by volume. Work out the volume of the shape, then add a little for waste.

Slab or footing: volume = length × width × thickness
Round column or hole: volume = 3.1416 × (diameter ÷ 2)² × height
Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27 · Cubic meters = cubic yards × 0.7646

Imperial example. A 20 ft × 10 ft patio, 4 inches thick: 20 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 66.7 cubic feet, which is 2.47 cubic yards. With 10% waste, order 2.72 cubic yards.

Metric example. A 6 m × 3 m slab, 10 cm thick: 6 × 3 × 0.10 = 1.8 m³. With 10% waste, order 1.98 m³, which most suppliers round to 2 m³.

Keep units consistent: convert inches to feet (divide by 12) or centimeters to meters (divide by 100) before multiplying. The calculator does this for you.

Local building codes set the real minimums, especially for footings, frost depth and reinforcement. Check with your building department before you pour anything structural.

Why add waste

Ground is never perfectly level, forms bow slightly, and some concrete stays in the mixer, pump or wheelbarrow. Add 5% for a formed slab on a well-prepared base and 10% for uneven ground or hand-dug footings. Running short in the middle of a pour leaves a weak cold joint, so a little extra is cheap insurance.

Ordering and pouring tips

  • Ready-mix is sold by the cubic yard in the US and by the cubic meter almost everywhere else. Many suppliers have a minimum load and charge a short-load fee below it.
  • Strength: 3,000 to 4,000 psi (20 to 30 MPa, or C20 to C30) suits most home slabs and driveways. Ask for air-entrained concrete where it freezes.
  • Base: 4 in (100 mm) of compacted gravel under a slab improves drainage and reduces cracking.
  • Reinforcement: wire mesh or rebar holds cracks together. Use the rebar weight calculator for the steel.
  • Control joints: cut or tool joints at spacings of about 24 to 30 times the slab thickness, so 8 to 10 ft for a 4 in slab.
  • Curing: keep the surface damp for at least 7 days. Concrete reaches most of its design strength at 28 days.
  • Weight: concrete weighs about 150 lb per cubic foot, or 2,400 kg per m³. Check what your vehicle and trailer can carry before collecting bags.

For the surface area of an irregular slab, measure it with the square footage calculator first.

Concrete questions

How do I calculate how much concrete I need?

Multiply length by width by thickness, all in the same unit. In feet, divide by 27 for cubic yards. A 20 by 10 ft slab 4 inches thick is 20 × 10 × 0.333 ÷ 27 = 2.47 cubic yards.

How many 80 lb bags of concrete are in a cubic yard?

About 45. Each 80 lb bag makes roughly 0.60 cubic feet, and a cubic yard is 27 cubic feet.

How many bags of concrete do I need for a 10 by 10 slab?

A 10 by 10 ft slab 4 inches thick is 1.23 cubic yards. That is 56 bags of 80 lb, or 62 with 10% waste.

How many 20 kg bags make a cubic meter?

Roughly 107 to 110 bags of 20 kg pre-mixed concrete, depending on the brand.

How thick should a concrete slab be?

4 inches (100 mm) for patios and paths, 4 to 5 inches for car driveways, and 5 to 6 inches for heavier vehicles. Follow your local building code.

How much does a cubic yard of concrete weigh?

About 4,050 lb, or roughly 2 US tons. A cubic meter weighs about 2,400 kg.

How much extra concrete should I order?

5% to 10% more than the measured volume. Use 10% for uneven ground, trenches and hand-dug holes.
