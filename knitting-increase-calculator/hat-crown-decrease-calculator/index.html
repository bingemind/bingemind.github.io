Where the changes go

In the round there are no edges. Every gap is a full gap, and the line repeats evenly all the way around.

Work K2tog over these stitch pairs, counting from the first stitch you have now: starting at stitch 8, 17, 26, 35, 44, 53, 62, 71, 80, 89, 98, 107.

  • Place a marker at the start of the round and work the pattern from there.

A hat crown closes in stages. This calculator plans one stage. It is set for 108 stitches and 12 decreases, which leaves 96 stitches for the next round.

Decrease in stages

A flat top needs decreases in the same places each time. A smooth, rounded top needs the decreases to move over a little each round. Use the calculator for the first round, then plan the next one with the new stitch count.

Many hats use 6, 8 or 12 decreases per decrease round. These numbers divide most hat sizes, which makes the crown look like a star or a spiral, depending on whether the decreases line up.

Moving to double-pointed needles

As the crown shrinks, the circle becomes too small for a circular needle. Switch to double-pointed needles or a longer cord with a magic loop when the stitches no longer reach around. Keep your marker at the start of the round.

Stitch count after each round

Each round removes the number you entered. From 108, with 12 decreases, the next round has 96. Then 84, and so on. The total under the pattern line shows the new count.

Questions

How many decreases should a hat crown have?

Many patterns use 6, 8 or 12 per decrease round. More decreases give a flatter top and fewer give a taller one.

Should the decreases line up from round to round?

If they line up, the lines show as spokes, like a star. If you shift them by a stitch each round, the top looks smoother.
