How to increase 8 stitches evenly across 50 stitches

To increase 8 stitches evenly across 50 stitches on a flat row, work: K3, M1, (K6, M1) 3 times, (K7, M1) twice, (K6, M1) twice, K3. You end with 58 stitches.

Pattern line

K3, M1, (K6, M1) 3 times, (K7, M1) twice, (K6, M1) twice, K3.

Check: 8 M1 and 50 K. You end with 58 stitches.

M1 (make one): lift the strand between two stitches with the left needle, then knit it through the back loop.

Where the changes go

Flat row: the first and last runs (3 and 3) are about half a gap, so no new stitch sits on the edge.

M1 after stitch 3, 9, 15, 21, 28, 35, 41, 47 (counted on the 50 you have now).

    50 stitches do not divide by 8. That is why a quick guess often leaves the new stitches bunched on one side. The line below spaces them with the leftover split at the ends.

    The math

    50 ÷ 8 is 6 with 2 left over. So most gaps are 6 stitches, and two of them are 7. On a flat row, the first and last runs are about half a gap, so the new stitches sit away from the edges.

    You end with 58 stitches. The check line under the result confirms 8 increases and 50 plain stitches.

    When this comes up

    This case is common in a sleeve or a body, where a pattern says to increase to the next size or to the stitch count for a ribbing change. If your pattern gives the final count (58) and not the number to add, use I know: Final stitch count.

    The same case in the round

    With 50 stitches and 8 increases in the round, the line is:

    (K6, M1) 6 times, (K7, M1) twice.

    Check: 8 M1 and 50 K. You end with 58 stitches.
