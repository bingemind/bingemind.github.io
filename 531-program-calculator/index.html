What a 5/3/1 calculator does

A 5/3/1 calculator turns your lifting numbers into a full four-week plan. You enter the squat, bench press, deadlift and overhead press. The tool gives every set for every week, with the weight on the bar and the plates to load on each side. It rounds each set to a weight your plates can make, so you do not do the math between sets.

How to use the calculator

  1. Pick pounds or kilograms.
  2. Choose if you enter a one-rep max (1RM) or a training max (TM). With a 1RM, the tool sets the TM to 90 percent of it. You can switch to 85 percent.
  3. Type your four numbers. The tables update as you type.
  4. Pick the week order, the main sets and the supplemental plan. Turn warm-up sets, joker sets and the deload week on or off.
  5. Open Bar and plates. Set your bar and turn off any plate your gym does not have.
  6. Tap a week to see its sets. Tap Print sheet for a paper copy of all four weeks.

The four weeks of the cycle

Each lift has one main day a week. Every percent is a percent of the TM. The last set of weeks 1 to 3 is an "as many reps as you can" set, shown with a plus sign. Stop one or two reps before your form breaks.

WeekSet 1Set 2Set 3
1 (5s)65% × 575% × 585% × 5+
2 (3s)70% × 380% × 390% × 3+
3 (5/3/1)75% × 585% × 395% × 1+
4 (deload)40% × 550% × 560% × 5

The 3/5/1 order swaps weeks 1 and 2. The 5s PRO option keeps the same percents but makes every set 5 reps, with no top set to failure.

Training max: the one number that matters

Training max = one-rep max × 0.90, rounded to the nearest 5 lb (2.5 kg). A 315 lb squat 1RM gives a TM of 285 lb, because 315 × 0.90 = 283.5, and 283.5 rounds to 285. A lower TM, such as 85 percent, makes the first cycles easier. The TM is not your true max on purpose. It leaves room to add reps and to progress for many cycles.

Supplemental work: BBB, FSL and SSL

Supplemental sets come after the main sets. They add volume. All three plans skip the deload week.

PlanSets and repsWeight
BBB5 × 1050 to 60% of the TM
FSL5 × 5The first main set (65, 70 or 75%)
SSL5 × 5The second main set (75, 80 or 85%)

The guides we checked give BBB as 50 to 60 percent of the TM. Many lifters start at 50 percent and move to 60 percent only when 5 × 10 feels easy.

Joker sets and the deload week

Joker sets are optional extra sets after a strong top set. One guide describes them as adding 5 to 10 percent above the top set, for 1 to 3 reps, and says they are earned, not expected. This tool shows two sets, at +5 and +10 percent of the TM, with 3 reps (1 rep in the 5/3/1 week). Stopping after one set, or after none, is fine. Advice that we found says to skip them in the 5s week, so the tool leaves them out there unless you turn that on. Skip them on any day your bar speed is slow. The deload week uses light weights so your body can recover before the next cycle. Leave it in unless you have a reason to remove it.

After the cycle: raise the training max

When week 4 is done, add 5 lb (2.5 kg) to the bench press and overhead press TMs, and 10 lb (5 kg) to the squat and deadlift TMs. The calculator shows the new numbers under the sets. If you missed the minimum reps on a top set, many lifters lower the TM by about 10 percent and build back up. Do not rush the jumps.

How the plate rounding works

The smallest plate sets the step. With 2.5 lb plates, a barbell moves in 5 lb steps. A set of 213.75 lb cannot be built, so the tool picks 215 lb. If two loads are equally close, it picks the heavier one. A weight under the bar becomes the empty bar. Switch on the 1.25 lb or 1.25 kg plate to get steps of 2.5 lb or 2.5 kg.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 5/3/1 program?

It is a strength program built on four lifts: squat, bench press, deadlift and overhead press. Each lift gets one main day a week. Over four weeks the sets move from 5s to 3s to 5/3/1, then a light deload week. After the cycle you add a small amount to each training max and start again.

What is a training max and how do I find mine?

The training max (TM) is the number every percentage is based on. It is usually 90 percent of your one-rep max. Use 85 percent if you are new to the program or want an easier start. If you do not know your one-rep max, pick a weight you can lift for 3 to 5 clean reps, then use a one-rep max chart to estimate it.

What is a 531 calculator?

A 531 calculator (also written 5/3/1 or 5-3-1) turns your training max into the weights for each week of the program: sets, reps, percents and the plates to load. This one also rounds to your plates and prints a sheet.

What is the difference between 5/3/1 and 3/5/1?

The weights are the same. 3/5/1 swaps the first two weeks, so you do the 3s week first and the 5s week second. Week 3 is still the 5/3/1 week and week 4 is still the deload. Some lifters like the heavier week early.

What does 5s PRO mean?

In 5s PRO you do 5 reps on every main set, in every week. There is no last set to failure. The weights are the same as the standard cycle. It leaves more energy for the supplemental work. The deload week stays at 5 reps as well.

What are BBB, FSL and SSL?

They are supplemental sets you do after the main sets. BBB (Boring But Big) is 5 sets of 10 at a fixed percent of the TM, 50 to 60 percent in the guides we checked, often 50. FSL (First Set Last) is 5 sets of 5 at the weight of the first main set. SSL (Second Set Last) is 5 sets of 5 at the weight of the second main set. They are skipped in the deload week.

What are joker sets?

Joker sets are optional extra sets after the top set. A guide we checked describes them as 5 to 10 percent above the top set, for 1 to 3 reps. Here you get two sets, at +5 and +10 percent of the TM, for 3 reps (1 rep in the 5/3/1 week). They are left out of the 5s week unless you turn that on. Use them only when the top set felt good and your form was clean. You can switch them off.

How much do I add to my training max after a cycle?

Add 5 lb (2.5 kg) to the bench press and overhead press, and 10 lb (5 kg) to the squat and deadlift. The calculator shows the new numbers under the cycle and lets you use them with one tap.

Is this the official calculator from the author of the book?

No. This is an independent tool and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by the author or the publisher of the book. It follows the published percentages and set and rep scheme.
