How solar system size is calculated

Yearly use (kWh) = monthly bill ÷ price per kWh × 12
System size (kW) = yearly use ÷ (peak sun hours × 365 × system efficiency)
Number of panels = system size × 1,000 ÷ panel watts, rounded up

Example: a 150 bill at 0.18 per kWh is 833 kWh a month, or 10,000 kWh a year. With 4.5 peak sun hours and 80% efficiency, each kW of panels makes 4.5 × 365 × 0.8 = 1,314 kWh a year. You need 10,000 ÷ 1,314 = 7.6 kW, which is 19 panels of 420 W.

What peak sun hours means

One peak sun hour is one hour of sunlight at 1,000 watts per square meter. It is a measure of total daily solar energy, not hours of daylight. The value depends on latitude and climate, and the calculator starts with a national average.

What decides the payback time

  • Your electricity price. The higher it is, the more each solar kWh saves. Payback is fastest where power is expensive, such as Germany, the UK, Australia and California.
  • How much solar power you use yourself. Power used at home saves the full retail price. Power exported is often paid at a lower feed-in or export rate.
  • Incentives. Enter any rebate or tax credit as a percentage. Examples include Australia's small-scale technology certificates, India's PM Surya Ghar subsidy, and 0% VAT on home installations in the UK. The US federal residential clean energy credit is not allowed for expenditures made after 31 December 2025, though some state and utility rebates remain.
  • Electricity price rises. If grid prices go up, your savings grow each year.
  • Roof direction and shade. A roof facing the equator at a moderate tilt gives the most. East or west faces produce roughly 15% to 20% less, and shade can cut output sharply.

Typical home payback is about 4 to 7 years in Australia and India, 6 to 10 in much of Europe, and 7 to 12 in the US. Panels usually carry a 25-year performance warranty.

Batteries, net metering and panel life

Net metering credits exported power at or near the retail rate, so the grid acts like a free battery. Where it applies, the calculator's savings are close to reality. Where export rates are low, you gain more by shifting use to daylight hours or adding a battery.

Batteries let you use solar power in the evening and provide backup, but they add substantially to the cost and usually lengthen the payback. They make most sense with low export rates, time-of-use tariffs or unreliable grids.

Degradation. Panels lose around 0.5% of output a year, which the 25-year figures include. Inverters often need replacing once, after 10 to 15 years.

Roof space. A modern 420 W panel is about 1.95 m² (21 sq ft). The result shows the area for panels alone; allow extra for setbacks, vents and walkways.

Solar questions

How many solar panels do I need?

Divide your yearly electricity use in kWh by the yearly output of one panel. A 420 W panel in a place with 4.5 peak sun hours makes about 550 kWh a year, so a home using 10,000 kWh needs about 19 panels.

How do I calculate solar system size?

Divide yearly kWh by peak sun hours × 365 × 0.8. For 10,000 kWh a year and 4.5 sun hours, that is about 7.6 kW.

How long do solar panels take to pay for themselves?

Commonly 4 to 12 years depending on the country, electricity price, incentives and how much of the power you use yourself. Divide the net cost by the first-year savings for a quick estimate.

How much roof space do solar panels need?

About 2 square meters (21 square feet) per panel. A 6 kW system of 15 panels needs roughly 30 m² or 320 sq ft, plus edge clearances.

What are peak sun hours?

A measure of daily solar energy: one peak sun hour equals 1 kWh of sunlight per square meter. Most inhabited places get between 2.5 and 6.5 a day on average.

Do solar panels work on cloudy days?

Yes, at reduced output, typically 10% to 25% of a clear day. The yearly average sun hours already include cloudy days.

Is there still a US federal solar tax credit?

No. Under the 2025 tax law (Public Law 119-21) the 30% residential clean energy credit is not allowed for expenditures made after 31 December 2025. State, local and utility incentives may still apply, so enter those if you qualify.
