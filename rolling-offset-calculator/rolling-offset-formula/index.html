The formulas

  • True offset = square root of (rise squared + roll squared)
  • Travel = true offset / sin(angle)
  • Run = true offset / tan(angle)
  • Multiplier = 1 / sin(angle)
  • Shrink = true offset x tan(angle / 2)
  • Cut length = travel - 2 x take-off
  • Fitting roll angle from level = arctangent(rise / roll)

Where they come from

Look at the offset from the end of the pipe. The rise and the roll are the two legs of a right triangle. The true offset is the long side.

Now look at the true offset from the side. It is one leg of a second right triangle. The travel is the long side, and the fitting angle sits between the travel and the run. That gives the sine and the tangent in the formulas.

Rolling offset formula worked step by step

Take a rise of 12 inches, a roll of 9 inches and 45 degree fittings. True offset = square root of (12 x 12 + 9 x 9) = square root of 225 = 15.00 inches.

Travel = 15.00 / sin 45 = 21.21 inches, or about 21 3/16". Run = 15.00 / tan 45 = 15.00 inches. For pipe, subtract the take-off of each fitting from the travel to get the cut length.

The fitting roll angle

When you set the two fittings, they must lie in the plane of the true offset. For a rise of 12 and a roll of 9, that plane tilts 53.1 degrees from level, or 36.9 degrees from plumb. The tool shows both numbers.

Questions

How do you calculate a rolling offset?

Find the true offset: the square root of rise squared plus roll squared. Divide it by the sine of the fitting angle to get the travel. For a 12 inch rise, a 9 inch roll and 45 degree fittings, the true offset is 15.00 inches and the travel is 21.21 inches.

What is the rolling offset formula?

The true offset is the square root of rise squared plus roll squared. The travel is the true offset divided by the sine of the fitting angle.

Is the rolling offset formula the same for pipe and conduit?

Yes. Pipe adds a take-off for each fitting. Conduit adds shrink for the bends.
