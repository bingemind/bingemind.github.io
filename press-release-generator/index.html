    The standard press release format

    Editors read a lot of releases. A familiar layout helps them find the news fast. This is the usual order, from top to bottom:

    1. Release line. "FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE", or "EMBARGOED UNTIL" with a date and time.
    2. Media contact. A name, a phone number or email, and the organization.
    3. Headline. One line that states the news.
    4. Subheadline. Optional. One more line of detail.
    5. Dateline. The city in capitals, the state or country, and the date, in front of the first paragraph.
    6. Body. The news first, then details, then a quote, then the facts a reader needs.
    7. About section. A short, fixed paragraph about the organization. It is often called the boilerplate.
    8. End mark. "###" or "-30-" in the center, so the editor knows the release is complete.

    News style dates

    Many newsrooms write dates in a short form. Long month names get a short form with a period: Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov. and Dec. March, April, May, June and July stay in full. So the dateline reads "MILLBROOK, Oregon, Nov. 10, 2026". Times use a.m. and p.m., and 12:00 is "noon". The formatter writes the date and time this way from what you pick, so you do not have to remember the rules.

    Write the first paragraph first

    Many readers stop after the first paragraph. Put the news there: who, what, when, where and why, in about 45 words or fewer. Save the history and the background for later paragraphs. The checks count the words in your first paragraph and tell you when it runs long.

    Quotes

    A quote adds a human voice. Use words the person really said, and get their approval before you send the release. Give the full name and role the first time. The formatter does not write or change quotes. It keeps your text as you typed it.

    The About section

    The About section is the same in every release your organization sends. Say what the organization does, where it works and since when. Keep it to two to four sentences. Write it once and the formatter keeps it in this browser for your next release.

    Embargoes

    An embargo asks reporters to hold the story until a set time. Give the date, the time and the time zone, for example "EMBARGOED UNTIL NOV. 12, 2026, 9 A.M. ET". Only use an embargo when you have a real reason, such as an event or a report that comes out at a set time.

    How long should it be?

    Most releases fit on one or two pages. If yours runs long, cut background and move detail to a fact sheet or a web page. The checks warn you when the body passes 800 words.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the standard press release format?

    From top to bottom: the release line (for immediate release or an embargo), the media contact, the headline, an optional subheadline, the dateline with the city and date, the body, the About section, and an end mark such as ###.

    Does this tool write the press release for me?

    No. You write every word. The formatter puts your text in the standard order, formats the date and checks for missing parts. It does not add facts or quotes.

    How do I write the date in a press release?

    Many newsrooms use a short month with a period for long month names, such as Nov. 10, 2026 or Sept. 5, 2026. March, April, May, June and July are written in full. The formatter does this for you from the date you pick.

    What does ### mean at the end of a press release?

    It marks the end of the release, so an editor knows nothing is missing. Some people use -30- instead. You can pick either one.

    What is an embargo?

    An embargo asks reporters not to publish before a set date and time. Choose "Embargoed until a date" and the release line shows the date, time and time zone you enter.

    How long should a press release be?

    Most fit on one or two pages. Keep the first paragraph short, about 45 words or fewer, and answer who, what, when, where and why. The checks warn you when the first paragraph or the body runs long.

    Is my text saved or sent anywhere?

    Your text is saved only in this browser, so it is still there when you come back. Nothing you type is sent to us. Press Clear all to remove it.
