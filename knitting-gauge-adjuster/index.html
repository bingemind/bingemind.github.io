        How to use the knitting gauge calculator

        1. Knit a swatch at least 6 in (15 cm) wide. Wash and dry it the way you will treat the finished piece.
        2. Count the stitches and rows over 4 in (10 cm) in the middle of the swatch. Type them under "Your gauge".
        3. Type the gauge from the pattern under "Pattern gauge". Use the unit the pattern uses.
        4. Add each number from the pattern that you want to change: the cast on, row counts, and shaping such as "decrease every 6th row 8 times".
        5. If the pattern has a stitch repeat, such as "a multiple of 4 plus 2", set it so the new counts still fit the pattern.
        6. Read your new numbers. Open "Show the steps" to check the sum. Then copy or print your numbers.

        What knitting gauge means

        Gauge is the number of stitches and rows in a set width and height of knitted fabric. A pattern might say "20 stitches and 28 rows = 4 in in stockinette stitch". If your swatch has more stitches in those 4 inches, your stitches are smaller. If you follow the pattern numbers as written, the piece comes out smaller than the pattern says.

        You can fix gauge in two ways. You can change needle size and knit a new swatch. Or you can keep your fabric and change the numbers in the pattern. This calculator does the second job. It works for crochet too, because the sums are the same.

        The formula for a new stitch count

        The calculator first turns each gauge into stitches per inch. Then it finds the width the pattern wants and multiplies by your gauge.

        • Pattern gauge: 20 stitches ÷ 4 in = 5 stitches per inch.
        • Your gauge: 22 stitches ÷ 4 in = 5.5 stitches per inch.
        • The pattern casts on 88 stitches: 88 ÷ 5 = 17.6 in wide.
        • At your gauge: 17.6 × 5.5 = 96.8 stitches.
        • Rounded to the nearest whole stitch: 97. Rounded to a multiple of 4 plus 2: 98.

        Your piece will then be 98 ÷ 5.5 = 17.82 in wide, which is 0.22 in wider than the pattern. The calculator shows this check for every stitch count, so you can see the cost of rounding.

        If one gauge is in centimetres, the calculator divides the width by 2.54 first. 10 cm is 3.94 in, not 4 in, so it does not treat them as the same.

        Rows and row gauge

        Row counts change the same way, with row gauge. If the pattern has 28 rows over 4 in and you have 30, then 14 rows becomes 14 × 30 ÷ 28 = 15 rows. Many patterns do not give a row count. They say "work until the piece measures 10 in". A length like that does not change, so you do not need to convert it.

        Turn on "Keep row counts even" when you want to end on the same side of the work as the pattern.

        Shaping: every Nth row

        Shaping is the hard part. "Decrease every 6th row 8 times" uses 48 rows and removes 8 sets of stitches. At a new gauge, both numbers change. The rows change with your row gauge, and the number of decreases changes with your stitch gauge, because you have more or fewer stitches to remove.

        The calculator works out the new total rows and the new number of decreases. Then it spreads the decreases over the rows as evenly as it can. With the gauges above, 48 rows become 51 rows and 8 decreases become 9. The new plan is "every 5th row 3 times, then every 6th row 6 times", which uses 3 × 5 + 6 × 6 = 51 rows. Always check the stitch count after shaping against the next number in your pattern.

        When to change needles instead

        Small differences are easy to fix with numbers. When your gauge is more than about 15% away from the pattern, the fabric also feels different. It may be too stiff or too loose for the design. In that case, try a different needle size first. The calculator shows a note when the difference is this large.

        Tips for a good swatch

        • Knit the swatch in the stitch pattern the gauge uses.
        • Measure in the middle, away from the edges.
        • Measure after washing and drying, the way you will treat the finished piece.
        • Count half stitches. 21.5 stitches is a real gauge, and the calculator accepts it.

        Frequently asked questions

        How do I adjust a knitting pattern for a different gauge?

        Find the width the pattern wants by dividing its stitch count by the pattern's stitches per inch. Multiply that width by your stitches per inch. Round to a whole stitch, or to the pattern's stitch repeat. This calculator does the steps for every number in your list.

        What if my gauge has more stitches than the pattern?

        Your stitches are smaller, so you need more of them. All stitch counts go up. If you have fewer stitches than the pattern, all stitch counts go down.

        Do I need to change the row count too?

        Only when the pattern gives rows. If it says "work until the piece measures 10 in", keep the length as written. If it gives a number of rows or a shaping rate, convert them with your row gauge.

        Can I mix inches and centimetres?

        Yes. Pick the unit for each gauge. The calculator divides cm by 2.54 before it compares the gauges.

        How does it round to a stitch repeat?

        Set "a multiple of 4 plus 2", for example. The calculator picks the closest count that fits the repeat, such as 94, 98 or 102. When two counts are the same distance away, it picks the larger one, which gives a little more ease.

        Does it work for crochet?

        Yes. Gauge works the same way for crochet stitches and rows.

        Is my data saved or sent anywhere?

        Your numbers are saved only in this browser, so they come back next time. Nothing you type is sent to us.
