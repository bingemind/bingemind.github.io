    What is a GZCLP calculator?

    A GZCLP calculator keeps the numbers of the GZCLP program for you. It shows which lift you do today, how many sets and reps, and how much weight is on the bar. After each workout it applies the progression rules, so you do not need a spreadsheet.

    GZCLP is a beginner strength program. It is based on the GZCL method by Cody LeFever and is described in the r/Fitness Wiki. You train four lifts, the squat, bench press, overhead press and deadlift, and a few accessory lifts. Each workout has three tiers.

    How the three tiers work

    T1 is the heavy lift of the day. T2 is a second lift with lighter weight and more reps. T3 is light accessory work. Each tier has its own sets, reps and rules for failure.

    TierStage 1Stage 2Stage 3Rest
    T15 × 3+6 × 2+10 × 1+3 to 5 min
    T23 × 103 × 83 × 62 to 3 min
    T33 × 15+No later stages1 to 1.5 min

    A plus sign means the last set is "as many reps as possible" (AMRAP). Stop one or two reps before you fail, and watch for a clear drop in bar speed.

    How to use this GZCLP calculator

    1. Under Your program, choose pounds or kilograms and type the weight for each T1, T2 and T3 lift. If you know your 5-rep max, type it and the tool sets T1 to 85 percent of it.
    2. Pick your bar and the plates your gym has. The tool shows what to load on each side.
    3. Do the workout. For each T1 and T2 lift, choose Pass or Fail. For T3, enter the reps you did on the last set.
    4. Press Log this session. The tool updates every weight and moves to the next day.

    The line under each lift shows what happens next time after a pass and after a fail, so you can see the plan before you start.

    Worked example

    You squat 135 lb for 5 × 3+ in T1. You get at least 3 reps on every set, so you pass. The squat is a lower body lift, so it goes up 10 lb. Next time you squat 145 lb for 5 × 3+.

    A few weeks later you squat 185 lb and fail a set. You do not add weight. You stay at 185 lb and move to 6 × 2+. If you fail again, you move to 10 × 1+ at 185 lb. If you fail that too, the cycle resets.

    GZCLP reset: what happens when you fail

    • T1: a fail moves you to the next stage at the same weight. After a fail at 10 × 1+, test a new 5-rep max and restart 5 × 3+ at 85 percent of it. Without a test, this tool uses 85 percent of the weight you failed with.
    • T2: a fail moves you from 3 × 10 to 3 × 8 to 3 × 6 at the same weight. After a fail at 3 × 6, restart 3 × 10 at the last 3 × 10 weight plus 15 lb (7.5 kg). The source suggests 15 to 20 lb.
    • T3: add weight when you reach 25 or more reps on the last set. Otherwise keep the weight.

    Starting weights and warm-ups

    The wiki does not give starting weights, so these are this tool's suggestions. Start T1 near a weight you could lift for 5 reps (85 percent of your 5-rep max is common, and you can type your 5-rep max under Your program). Start T2 at a weight you could lift for 10 reps with a few to spare. Start T3 at a weight you can lift for 15 or more reps. When in doubt, start lighter. The first weeks should feel easy.

    The program does not define warm-up sets. A common habit is to do a set with the empty bar, then two or three lighter sets of 3 to 5 reps on the way up to your first T1 weight. These sets are not part of the progression, and the tool does not track them. AMRAP means "as many reps as possible": stop one or two reps before you would fail.

    Frequently asked questions

    What does GZCLP stand for?

    GZCLP is short for GZCL Linear Progression. It is a beginner version of the GZCL method by Cody LeFever. It uses three tiers of lifts: T1 for heavy sets, T2 for moderate sets and T3 for light accessory work.

    How many days a week is GZCLP?

    Three or four days a week. The program has four workouts (Day 1 to Day 4). You run them in order and leave at least one rest day between workouts. On three days a week the days drift across the calendar, for example Monday, Wednesday and Friday.

    What counts as a fail on T1?

    In the 5 × 3+ stage (also written 5x3+) you pass if you complete at least 3 reps on every set, and the last set is as many reps as you can do. In 6 × 2+ the minimum is 2 reps per set. In 10 × 1+ the minimum is 1 rep per set. If you cannot reach that minimum on every set, you fail the session.

    What do I do after I fail the last stage of T1?

    The source rules say to test a new 5-rep max and restart at 5 × 3+ with 85 percent of it. If you do not test, this calculator estimates 85 percent of the weight you failed with. You can type any weight in the Your program panel to replace the estimate.

    What happens after I fail T2 at 3 × 6?

    You restart at 3 × 10. The new weight is the last weight you used in the 3 × 10 stage plus 15 to 20 lb. This calculator adds 15 lb (7.5 kg) and rounds to 5 lb (2.5 kg).

    When do I add weight to T3?

    Add weight when you get 25 or more reps on the last set of 3 × 15+. Fewer reps than that keeps the weight the same. This calculator adds 5 lb (2.5 kg) for both T3 lifts.

    How much weight does each lift go up?

    Upper body lifts (bench press, overhead press, lat pulldown, dumbbell row) go up 5 lb or 2.5 kg after a pass. Lower body lifts (squat and deadlift) go up 10 lb or 5 kg. The wiki gives these jumps in pounds. The kilogram jumps are the same numbers in kilograms. The wiki does not set a jump for T3, so this tool uses 5 lb (2.5 kg).

    Is my data saved?

    Yes, on your device only. The calculator keeps your weights and session log in your browser. Nothing is sent to a server. If you clear your browser data, it is gone, so print your sessions if you want a paper copy.
