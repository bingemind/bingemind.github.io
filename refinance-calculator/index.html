How to tell if refinancing is worth it

Refinancing replaces your loan with a new one. It costs money upfront, so it only pays if you keep the loan long enough.

Monthly saving = current payment − new payment
Break-even months = closing costs ÷ monthly saving

Example: 280,000 left at 7.3% with 27 years to go is 1,980.97 a month. A new 30-year loan at 6.3% is 1,733.12, a saving of 247.85 a month. With 5,000 in costs, you break even after 21 months.

The trap: a lower payment that costs more

If you restart a 30-year term, you make payments for longer. A lower monthly payment can still add up to more interest in total. The result always shows lifetime savings or extra cost, and warns you when the payment falls but the total rises. Choosing a new term close to the years you have left avoids this.

When refinancing usually makes sense

  • You can cut the rate by about 0.75 to 1 point or more and will stay past the break-even month.
  • You want a shorter term. Moving from 30 to 15 years raises the payment but removes a large amount of interest.
  • Your fixed period is ending (UK, Canada, Australia, Ireland). Moving to a new deal instead of the lender's standard variable rate is the normal way to keep costs down.
  • You can drop mortgage insurance because your home has gained value and you now have 20% equity.
  • You are switching from a variable to a fixed rate for certainty.

When it usually does not

  • You plan to move before the break-even month.
  • You are far into your current loan, so most of each payment already goes to principal.
  • There is a large early repayment charge on your current deal.

Enter the total of all of these as closing costs. If you add them to the loan, choose "Add to the new loan" so the interest on them is counted.

Cash-out refinancing and points

Cash-out refinancing means borrowing more than you owe and taking the difference in cash. To model it, raise the balance by the cash you take. Remember you are converting home equity into debt.

Discount points are fees paid upfront for a lower rate, usually 1% of the loan for about 0.25 points off the rate. Add the cost of points to closing costs and enter the lower rate. If the break-even month is later than you expect to keep the loan, skip the points.

Refinancing questions

How do I calculate the break-even point on a refinance?

Divide the total closing costs by the monthly saving. With 5,000 in costs and a saving of 247.85 a month, you break even after about 21 months.

Is it worth refinancing for a 1% lower rate?

Often yes, if you will keep the loan beyond the break-even month and you do not stretch the term so far that total interest rises. Check the lifetime savings line in the result.

Why does my lower payment cost more overall?

Because the new loan runs for more years than you had left. Spreading the balance over a longer time lowers each payment but adds more months of interest.

Should I roll closing costs into the new loan?

It saves cash today, but you pay interest on the costs for the life of the loan. Paying upfront is cheaper in total if you can afford it.

What is the difference between refinancing and remortgaging?

They mean the same thing. Remortgage is the UK term for replacing your mortgage with a new one, either with a new lender or as a product transfer with your current lender.

How soon can I refinance?

There is usually no legal minimum, but lenders often want six months of payments, and fixed-rate deals outside the US can carry early repayment charges until the fixed period ends.
