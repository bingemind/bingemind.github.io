Margin and markup formulas

Profit = selling price − cost
Margin = profit ÷ selling price × 100
Markup = profit ÷ cost × 100
Price for a target margin = cost ÷ (1 − margin ÷ 100)
Price for a target markup = cost × (1 + markup ÷ 100)

Example: an item costs 60 and sells for 100. Profit is 40. Margin is 40 ÷ 100 = 40%. Markup is 40 ÷ 60 = 66.7%.

The mistake that costs sellers money

Margin and markup are not interchangeable. If you want a 40% margin and add 40% to your cost, you price the item at 84 and earn a margin of only 28.6%. To get a 40% margin you need a 66.7% markup. The table in the result converts between the two for your own cost.

Gross margin, net margin and break-even

  • Gross margin covers only the direct cost of the item: materials, stock, packaging, payment and marketplace fees, shipping you pay.
  • Net margin also subtracts overheads such as rent, salaries, software and advertising. Enter them as fixed costs to see it.
  • Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ profit per item. With 2,000 of fixed costs and 40 profit per item, you need to sell 50 items a month before you earn anything.

What discounts really cost

A discount comes straight out of profit, not out of cost. On an item with a 40% margin, a 10% discount cuts profit per item by a quarter, so you need to sell a third more units to make the same money. A 20% discount halves the profit and you need to sell twice as many. Enter a discount above to see the effect on your product.

Remember sales tax and VAT

Work out margin on prices before VAT, GST or sales tax, because that tax is collected for the government and is not your revenue. Use the sales tax and VAT calculator to strip tax from a shelf price first.

Margin and markup questions

How do I calculate profit margin?

Subtract cost from the selling price, divide by the selling price and multiply by 100. An item that costs 60 and sells for 100 has a 40% margin.

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Both use the same profit, but margin divides it by the selling price and markup divides it by the cost. A 50% markup equals a 33.3% margin, and a 50% margin equals a 100% markup.

How do I price a product for a 30% margin?

Divide the cost by 0.70. An item costing 35 needs a price of 50 to give a 30% margin. Adding 30% to the cost would give only a 23.1% margin.

What is a good profit margin?

It depends on the industry. A net margin around 10% is often described as healthy, 20% as high and 5% as low, but supermarkets run on 1% to 3% and software firms much more.

How do I calculate break-even units?

Divide your fixed costs by the profit on each item. With 2,000 of monthly fixed costs and 40 profit per item, break-even is 50 items a month.

Should I include VAT or sales tax in the price?

No. Calculate margin on the price before tax, since the tax is passed on to the government.
