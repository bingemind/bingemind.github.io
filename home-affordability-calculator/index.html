How lenders decide how much you can borrow

Lenders in different countries use two main approaches. Pick the one that matches yours under "Lender test" (it is set for your country automatically).

Debt-to-income ratios

Housing payment ≤ income × housing limit
Housing payment + other debts ≤ income × all-debts limit

The classic US version is the 28/36 rule: housing costs up to 28% of gross monthly income, and housing plus all other debt payments up to 36%. The calculator takes the lower of the two limits, subtracts insurance, HOA dues and property tax, and converts what is left into a loan at your rate and term.

Income multiples

Maximum loan = household income × multiple

UK lenders typically lend up to about 4.5 times income, and Irish rules allow 4 times for first-time buyers and 3.5 times for others. The home price is then the loan plus your deposit.

Affordable for the bank is not always comfortable for you

The lender's limit is the most you can borrow. Many households are more comfortable below it.

  • Budget on take-home pay. Ratios use income before tax. Check the payment against what actually reaches your account.
  • Keep an emergency fund of three to six months of expenses after paying the deposit and fees.
  • Add the costs that are not in the payment: utilities, repairs (about 1% of the home's value a year), furniture and commuting.
  • Allow for closing costs: typically 2% to 5% in the US, plus stamp duty or land transfer tax in the UK, Australia, Canada and India.
  • Test a higher rate. The table in the result shows how the affordable price moves when the rate changes by half a point or a point.

How to afford more house, safely

  • Pay down monthly debts. Clearing a 400 a month car payment can raise the loan you qualify for by tens of thousands.
  • Save a larger deposit. It adds to the price directly and can remove mortgage insurance or unlock a better rate.
  • Improve your credit score before applying, to get a lower rate.
  • Look at first-time buyer schemes: FHA and state programmes (US), First Home Savings Account (Canada), Lifetime ISA (UK), First Home Guarantee (Australia) and PMAY (India).

Affordability questions

How much house can I afford on my salary?

Under the 28/36 rule, your housing payment can be up to 28% of gross monthly income. On 100,000 a year with 500 of other monthly debts, 60,000 saved and a 6.3% rate, that supports a home of roughly 364,000.

What is the 28/36 rule?

A US lending guideline: spend no more than 28% of gross monthly income on housing and no more than 36% on all debts including housing.

How many times my salary can I borrow in the UK?

Most UK lenders offer up to about 4.5 times household income, and some go higher for high earners or certain professions. The lender also checks your outgoings.

Does the calculator include property tax and insurance?

Yes. Both are subtracted from your limit before the loan is worked out, because lenders count them as part of the housing payment.

How does my down payment affect what I can afford?

It adds directly to the price you can pay, and a deposit of 20% or more avoids mortgage insurance in the US and Canada and usually earns a lower rate.

Why is the amount lower in Canada or Australia than the rate suggests?

Lenders there must test you at a higher rate than you actually pay. Enter the stress-test rate to see the lender’s limit, and your real rate to see the actual payment.
