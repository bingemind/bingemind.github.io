What is a mileage log?

A mileage log is a list of the trips you make in your car for work or other reasons you need to track. Each line shows the date, where you went, why you went and how far you drove. This mileage log maker keeps that list in your browser, adds up the miles by month and year, and prints a clean log or a CSV file for a spreadsheet.

How to use the mileage log maker

  1. Type the date, where you went, why you went and the miles. Or switch to odometer and type the start and end readings.
  2. Press Add trip. Repeat for each trip. Use Copy for a trip you make often.
  3. Check the rate table. Change a rate if your employer or the official rate uses a different number.
  4. Pick the year. Read your month and year totals.
  5. Press Print log, or Download CSV for a spreadsheet.
  6. Download a CSV backup now and then. You can open it here again later.

What to write for each trip

The IRS guide on car expenses (Publication 463, chapter 5) says a record of business use of a car should show:

  • the date of each trip,
  • your business destination,
  • the business purpose,
  • the miles for each business trip,
  • the total miles for the year.

The same guide says to write each trip down at or near the time you make it. A log that you fill in each week counts as kept on time. Write the purpose in plain words, such as "Client meeting, kitchen remodel quote" or "Supply pickup for the shop".

Read the guide itself for the full rules: IRS Publication 463. This page does not tell you what you can claim. Ask a tax professional.

Miles or odometer

You can type the miles for a trip, or type the odometer at the start and the end. The tool works out the miles. Odometer readings are good proof, so many drivers use them for long trips. Use Round trip when you drove there and back on the same route.

Rates change, so the rate is yours to set

The tool multiplies your miles by a rate for each trip. The rate depends on the date of the trip. For 2026 the official business rate changed in the middle of the year:

Trips fromBusiness rate
January 1 to June 30, 202672.5 cents per mile
July 1 to December 31, 202676 cents per mile

Source: IRS standard mileage rates, checked on November 10, 2026. The rate for 2027 was not published when this page was written. Check the current official rate before you use these numbers, and add a new row to the rate table when it changes. If your employer pays you back at its own rate, type that rate instead.

Print, PDF and CSV

Print log makes a landscape page with your trips, oldest first, and a table of totals by month. To make a PDF, choose Save as PDF in the print window. Download CSV gives you a file that opens in any spreadsheet program. Print blank log gives you empty pages to keep in the car and fill in by hand.

Keep a backup

Your log lives in this browser on this device. Nobody else can see it, and it is not sent anywhere. That also means that if you clear your browser data or change phones, the log is gone. Download a CSV backup from time to time. You can open the backup here later to bring your trips back.

Tips for a log you can trust

  • Write each trip on the same day, or at least once a week.
  • Use the same words for trips you make often, so they are easy to find.
  • Write down the odometer on January 1 and December 31 each year.
  • Keep receipts for tolls and parking with your log.

Frequently asked questions

Is this mileage log free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no trip limit per month and no watermark. The only limit is 2,000 trips in one log.

Are my trips uploaded anywhere?

No. Your trips are saved only in this browser on this device. Nothing is sent to a server. Download a CSV backup so you do not lose them if you clear your browser data.

What rate does the tool use?

It uses the rate table on the page. The default rows are the official 2026 rates, checked on November 10, 2026, with a link to the source. You can change any rate or add a new one. Always check the current official rate.

Is this tax advice?

No. The tool keeps a record of your trips and multiplies miles by the rate you choose. Ask a tax professional what you can claim.

Can I print a blank mileage log?

Yes. Press Print blank log and choose how many pages. Each page has 25 rows for date, odometer, miles, places and purpose.

How do I save the log as a PDF?

Press Print log, then choose Save as PDF in the print window.

Can I open the log in a spreadsheet?

Yes. Download CSV gives a file that opens in any spreadsheet program. You can also open a CSV backup made by this tool to bring your trips back.
