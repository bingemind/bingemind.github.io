Percentage formulas

Percent means "out of one hundred". Every percentage question is one of a few formulas.

X% of Y = X ÷ 100 × Y
X is what percent of Y = X ÷ Y × 100
Percent change from X to Y = (Y − X) ÷ |X| × 100
Increase X by Y% = X × (1 + Y ÷ 100)
Decrease X by Y% = X × (1 − Y ÷ 100)
X is Y% of what = X ÷ (Y ÷ 100)
Percent difference = |X − Y| ÷ ((X + Y) ÷ 2) × 100

Worked examples

What is 15% of 80?

15 ÷ 100 × 80 = 12. A quick mental route: 10% of 80 is 8, 5% is half of that, 4, and 8 + 4 = 12.

20 is what percent of 80?

20 ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%.

Percent change from 50 to 75

(75 − 50) ÷ 50 × 100 = 50% increase. From 75 back to 50 is (50 − 75) ÷ 75 × 100 = −33.3%, a decrease of a third. The two are not the same size, because the starting number is different.

A price of 200 rises by 10%

200 × 1.10 = 220. If it then falls by 10%, the result is 220 × 0.90 = 198, not 200.

12 is 15% of what number?

12 ÷ 0.15 = 80. This is the reverse percentage, useful for finding an original price before a discount or tax.

Percent difference between 40 and 60

|40 − 60| ÷ 50 × 100 = 40%. Use this when neither number is the "before" value, for example when comparing two quotes.

Everyday uses

  • Discounts: 30% off 80 is 80 × 0.70 = 56. Use "Decrease X by Y%".
  • Tips: 18% of a 45 bill is 8.10. Use "What is X% of Y".
  • Pay rises: going from 52,000 to 55,000 is a 5.77% rise. Use "Percent change".
  • Exam scores: 42 out of 60 is 70%. Use "X is what percent of Y".
  • Tax and VAT: a total of 120 including 20% tax was 100 before tax. Use "X is Y% of what" with Y set to 120, or the sales tax and VAT calculator.
  • Investments: a share bought at 40 and now at 46 is up 15%.

Percent or percentage points?

If an interest rate goes from 4% to 5%, it has risen by one percentage point, but by 25 percent. News reports often mix the two up, and the difference matters.

A handy trick: X% of Y is always the same as Y% of X. So 8% of 50 is the same as 50% of 8, which is 4.

Percentage questions

How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

Divide the percentage by 100 and multiply by the number. 15% of 80 is 0.15 × 80 = 12.

How do I work out what percent one number is of another?

Divide the first number by the second and multiply by 100. 20 out of 80 is 20 ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%.

How do I calculate percentage increase?

Subtract the old value from the new one, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. From 50 to 75 is (75 − 50) ÷ 50 × 100 = 50%.

How do I calculate percentage decrease?

Use the same formula as an increase. A negative answer is a decrease. From 80 to 60 is (60 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = −25%, a 25% decrease.

How do I find the original price before a discount?

Divide the sale price by 1 minus the discount as a decimal. A price of 56 after 30% off was 56 ÷ 0.70 = 80.

What is the difference between percent change and percent difference?

Percent change compares a new value with an old one, so direction matters. Percent difference compares two values with their average, and is used when neither is the starting point.

Why does a 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease not get back to the start?

Because the second percentage is taken from a larger number. 100 up 50% is 150, and 150 down 50% is 75.
