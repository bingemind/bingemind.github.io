When to use 1:1:1

Use 1:1:1 when you want a starter that is ready soon. It is the usual choice to wake a starter from the fridge, to rescue a weak one, or to build starter for a bake the same day.

It also suits bakers who feed twice a day. The starter gets fresh food often, so it stays active and tends to taste more tangy.

How long a 1:1:1 feeding takes to peak

At 75 °F, a 1:1:1 feeding usually peaks in 3.5 to 5 hours. In a cool 68 °F kitchen, expect 4.5 to 7 hours. In a warm 82 °F kitchen, expect 2.5 to 4 hours. These are guides for an active starter at 100% hydration fed with white flour.

A small feeding spreads the yeast only a little, so the food runs out quickly. That is why the peak comes early and the starter can fall back before you notice.

A common mistake

Many bakers use 1:1:1 and then feed once a day. The starter peaks in the afternoon, falls, and sits hungry for hours. If you feed once a day, use 1:5:5 instead, or feed more often.

Quick answers

How much flour and water do I add for 1:1:1 with 20 g of starter?

Add 20 g of flour and 20 g of water to 20 g of starter. The jar holds 60 g after the feeding.

Is 1:1:1 the right ratio for me?

It is right if you can feed every 4 to 8 hours or want a quick wake-up. If you feed once a day, a larger ratio such as 1:5:5 fits better.
