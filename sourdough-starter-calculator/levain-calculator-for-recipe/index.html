How this differs from a normal feeding

In a normal feeding you choose how much starter to keep. For a levain you choose how much you need in the end. The calculator works backward from your total and gives the starter, flour and water that add up to it.

The amounts are rounded to whole grams and still add up to your total.

Timing the levain

Mix the levain a set number of hours before you mix the dough. At 75 °F, a 1:5:5 levain is ready in 8 to 12 hours. A 1:2:2 levain is ready in 5.5 to 8 hours. Use it when it has doubled and the top is domed.

Many recipes call for the levain at its peak. A levain that has fallen is more sour and less strong.

Build extra

Make a little more than the recipe needs, for example 10 g more. You lose some to the jar walls. The extra also becomes the starter you save for next time.

Quick answers

What is the difference between levain and starter?

A starter is the culture you keep alive. A levain is a portion you feed to build up for one bake. The math is the same.

Which ratio should I use for a levain?

Use 1:5:5 for an overnight levain and 1:2:2 for a few hours. Pick the one that peaks when you plan to mix the dough.
