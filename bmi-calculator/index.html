How BMI is calculated

Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
Imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²

Example: 70 kg and 1.70 m gives 70 ÷ (1.70 × 1.70) = 24.2. In imperial units, 154 lb and 5 ft 7 in (67 in) gives 703 × 154 ÷ 4,489 = 24.1.

Why Asian cut-off points are lower

Research reviewed by a WHO expert consultation found that people of South Asian, East Asian and South-East Asian background tend to develop type 2 diabetes and heart disease at a lower BMI than people of European background, partly because of a higher share of body fat at the same BMI.

For that reason several countries use lower action points:

CategoryWHO standardAsian action points
Healthy weight18.5 to 24.918.5 to 22.9
Overweight (increased risk)25 to 29.923 to 27.4
Obesity (high risk)30 and above27.5 and above

The calculator selects the Asian points automatically for countries where health authorities use them, such as India and Singapore, and you can switch between the two at any time. In the UK, NICE also advises the lower thresholds for people with South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean family backgrounds.

What BMI can and cannot tell you

BMI is quick and needs only a scale and a tape measure, which is why doctors use it as a first screen. It has clear limits:

  • It does not separate fat from muscle. A muscular athlete can have a BMI over 25 with low body fat.
  • It does not show where fat is carried. Fat around the waist carries more risk than fat on the hips.
  • Older adults lose muscle with age, so BMI can understate body fat.
  • Pregnancy: BMI is not used during pregnancy.
  • Children and teenagers need age- and sex-specific growth charts, not adult categories.

Add a waist measurement

A simple companion check is the waist-to-height ratio: keep your waist below half your height. For a person 170 cm tall, that is a waist under 85 cm. Measure midway between the bottom of your ribs and the top of your hips, after breathing out.

BMI questions

How do I calculate my BMI?

Divide your weight in kilograms by your height in metres squared. In imperial units, multiply your weight in pounds by 703 and divide by your height in inches squared.

What is a healthy BMI?

For most adults, 18.5 to 24.9. For people of Asian background, many health bodies treat 18.5 to 22.9 as the healthy range.

Is BMI accurate?

It is a useful screening measure for most adults, but it cannot tell muscle from fat or show where fat is carried. Doctors combine it with waist size, blood pressure and blood tests.

Is BMI different for men and women?

The formula and the adult categories are the same. Women generally have more body fat than men at the same BMI, which is one reason BMI is only a screening tool.

Can I use this BMI calculator for children?

No. Children and teenagers aged 2 to 19 are assessed with BMI-for-age percentile charts that take age and sex into account.

Why does the calculator show a different category for Asian cut-offs?

Health risks rise at a lower BMI in Asian populations, so overweight starts at 23 and obesity at 27.5 on that scale, compared with 25 and 30 on the standard one.

What is BMI prime?

Your BMI divided by the upper limit of the healthy range. A value of 1.0 is exactly at the limit, so 1.1 means you are 10% above it.
