How to calculate overtime pay

Overtime pay is your hourly rate times the overtime multiplier, times your overtime hours. Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), non-exempt workers get at least 1.5 times their regular rate for every hour over 40 in a workweek.

Overtime rate = hourly rate × 1.5
Overtime pay = overtime rate × hours over 40
Weekly pay = (hourly rate × regular hours) + overtime pay

Daily rules come first, then the weekly limit

In states with a daily rule, the calculator first marks hours over the daily limit as overtime (or double time). It then checks the week: regular hours past 40 become overtime, in day order. No hour is paid twice, which is how California's Division of Labor Standards Enforcement describes it.

No tax on overtime: how much you could deduct (2025–2028)

A federal income tax deduction now covers part of your overtime for tax years 2025 through 2028. Only the premium counts: the extra half in time and a half, for overtime the FLSA requires (hours over 40 in a workweek). If you earn $20 an hour and work 10 overtime hours, you are paid $300 for them, and $100 of that is the deductible premium.

  • The deduction is capped at $12,500 a year, or $25,000 for married couples filing jointly.
  • It starts to phase out above $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($300,000 joint).
  • Married couples must file jointly, and you need a valid Social Security number.
  • You can take it whether or not you itemize. It is claimed on Schedule 1-A.
  • From tax year 2026, your employer reports the amount on your W-2 in box 12 with code TT.
  • It lowers federal income tax only. Social Security, Medicare and most state taxes still apply.
  • Overtime paid only because of a state daily rule (for example California's 8-hour day) does not count unless those hours are also over 40 in the week.

Source: IRS questions and answers on the qualified overtime deduction. This is general information, not tax advice.

Overtime examples, exemptions and the regular rate

Example 1: federal overtime

You earn $20 an hour and work 45 hours in a week. The first 40 hours pay $800.00. The 5 overtime hours pay 5 × $30 = $150.00. Gross pay is $950.00.

Example 2: California daily overtime

You earn $20 an hour and work 10 hours a day, Monday to Friday. In California, hours over 8 in a day are overtime, so each day has 8 regular and 2 overtime hours. The week has 40 regular hours ($800.00) and 10 overtime hours at $30 ($300.00), for $1,100.00. A 7th consecutive day of work in a workweek has its own rules, with double time after 8 hours on that day.

Example 3: a bonus changes the overtime rate

You earn $18 an hour, work 45 hours, and receive a $90 production bonus for the week. Under the federal rule, a bonus that is promised or tied to production is added to pay before the regular rate is worked out. Straight-time pay is 45 × $18 = $810, plus the $90 bonus, which is $900. The regular rate is $900 ÷ 45 = $20.00. The overtime premium is half the regular rate for each overtime hour, so 5 × $10 = $50. Total pay is $950.00. A truly discretionary bonus is not included in the regular rate.

Who is not entitled to overtime

In the US, workers who are exempt from overtime are paid a salary at or above a set level and do certain kinds of executive, administrative or professional work, among other exemptions. A job title or a salary alone does not make someone exempt. The duties test also applies, and state rules can be stricter than the federal rule. Outside the US, managers and senior staff are often excluded from overtime rules, and some countries cap total overtime in a week, month or year.

Questions about hours that count

  • Paid time off: holiday, sick leave and vacation hours are usually not counted as hours worked for the 40-hour test, unless your contract or local law says so.
  • On-call and travel time: they count when you must stay at or near work or cannot use the time freely. Ordinary commuting does not count.
  • Comp time: private employers in the US generally cannot give time off in place of overtime pay. Public employers can in some cases.
  • Two jobs: hours at two separate employers are not added together. Hours for the same employer, or joint employers, are.
  • Pay frequency: overtime is paid on the regular payday for the period in which the workweek ends, or as soon as it can be calculated.

What to do if overtime is missing from your pay

Keep your own record of the hours you worked, using the time card calculator or a notebook. Compare it with your pay stub, and then ask your payroll team to explain any difference. If it is not resolved, you can contact your state labour office or the US Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division. Outside the US, use your national labour authority or a union. The estimate from this page is a reasonable starting point when you ask questions, but it is not legal advice.

Overtime questions

How is overtime calculated outside the United States?

Most countries set a weekly or daily limit and a premium on hours above it. Common limits are 40 to 48 hours a week or 8 to 9 hours a day, and common premiums are 1.25x to 2x. Choose your country in the header, check the multiplier against your contract, and enter your hours by day.

Does the UK have a legal overtime rate?

No. The United Kingdom has no statutory overtime premium. Your employment contract sets whether overtime is paid and at what rate. Use the custom rule: enter your contracted weekly hours and the multiplier your contract gives you.

How does overtime work in Canada?

Each province sets its own rule. Ontario pays 1.5x after 44 hours a week. British Columbia pays 1.5x after 8 hours a day or 40 a week and 2x after 12 hours a day. Alberta pays 1.5x after 8 hours a day or 44 a week. Quebec pays 1.5x after 40 hours a week.

How do I calculate overtime pay?

Multiply your hourly rate by 1.5 to get the overtime rate, then multiply that by your overtime hours. Under federal law, overtime hours are the hours over 40 in one workweek. Add your regular pay for the first 40 hours to get the week's total.

What is time and a half for $20 an hour?

Time and a half for $20 an hour is $30 an hour. Ten overtime hours at that rate pay $300 on top of your regular pay.

How much is 10 hours of overtime?

Ten hours of overtime pay 15 times your hourly rate. At $18 an hour that is $270, at $25 it is $375, and at $30 it is $450, before tax.

Is overtime paid after 8 hours a day?

Not under federal law, which only counts hours over 40 in a week. Some states add a daily rule: California, Alaska (employers with 4 or more employees) and Nevada (for workers paid under 1.5 times minimum wage) pay overtime after 8 hours in a day, and Colorado after 12. California also pays double time after 12 hours in a day.

Is overtime calculated weekly or biweekly?

Weekly. Even if you are paid every two weeks, each workweek is counted on its own. 30 hours one week and 50 the next gives 10 overtime hours, not zero. Use our time card calculator for a two-week view.

What is double time?

Double time is twice your regular hourly rate. Federal law does not require it. California requires it for hours over 12 in a workday and for hours over 8 on the seventh consecutive day of work in a workweek.

Is overtime tax free now?

Not fully. For tax years 2025 to 2028, a federal income tax deduction covers the premium part of FLSA-required overtime (the extra half in time and a half), up to $12,500 a year or $25,000 for joint filers, phasing out above $150,000 of income ($300,000 joint). Social Security and Medicare tax still apply, and state rules differ.

Is overtime taxed more?

No. Overtime is taxed at the same rates as the rest of your pay. A big overtime paycheck can have more withheld because payroll treats it as if you earned that much every period, but the extra comes back when you file your return if it was too much.

Can my employer make me work overtime?

Under federal law, yes. There is no limit on overtime hours for adults, and an employer can require it, as long as non-exempt workers are paid for it. Some states limit mandatory overtime for nurses and some other jobs.

Do salaried employees get overtime?

Some do. Salaried workers who are not exempt under the FLSA are owed overtime. To be exempt, most employees must earn at least $684 a week ($35,568 a year) and do executive, administrative or professional work. Some states set a higher salary level.

Do part-time workers get overtime?

Yes, if they work more than 40 hours in a workweek, or more than a state's daily limit. Part-time status does not change the overtime rules.

Do paid holidays and sick days count toward overtime?

Under federal law, overtime is based on hours actually worked, so paid time off usually does not count toward the 40 hours. Some employers and union contracts count it anyway. Enter only hours worked unless your policy says otherwise.
