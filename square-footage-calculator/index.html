How to calculate square footage

Measure the length and width of the room in feet and multiply them. If a measurement has inches, divide the inches by 12 and add them to the feet first: 12 feet 6 inches is 12.5 feet. For odd-shaped rooms, split the space into rectangles, add their areas, and subtract any cut-outs.

Rectangle = length × width
L-shape = (full length × full width) − (cut-out length × cut-out width)
Circle = 3.1416 × (diameter ÷ 2)²
Triangle = ½ × base × height
Trapezoid = (top + bottom) ÷ 2 × height

How much flooring to buy

Add 10% for waste with most plank flooring and carpet, 15% for tile or diagonal layouts, and 20% for herringbone. Divide the total by the square feet in one box and round up. Keep a spare box for future repairs.

Measuring a room and choosing how much to buy

Square footage tells you how much floor, wall or ceiling you have. It is the number behind almost every home project: flooring, paint, carpet, tile, sod and even rent. Getting it right avoids both a second trip to the store and a pile of leftover material.

How to measure

Use a tape measure and measure at the widest points, wall to wall, not along the baseboard. Measure twice and write the numbers down. If the room is not a perfect rectangle, divide it into simple shapes, calculate each one, and add them together. Subtract areas you will not cover, such as a kitchen island, a fireplace hearth or a built-in cabinet, if you are buying floor material.

How much extra to buy (waste)

  • Tile and straight-lay flooring: add about 10% for cuts and breakage.
  • Diagonal or patterned layouts: add 15% or more, since more pieces are trimmed.
  • Carpet: comes in fixed-width rolls, so ask the store to plan the layout. The waste depends on room shape and roll width.
  • Hardwood and laminate: add about 5% to 10%, plus a few extra boxes if the product may be discontinued.
  • Paint: one gallon usually covers roughly 350 to 400 square feet of wall, one coat. Check the label, because coverage depends on the paint and the surface.

Set the waste percentage in the calculator to see the quantity you should buy, and add a unit price to see the cost.

Wall area for paint

For paint you need wall area, not floor area. Add up the length of all walls (the perimeter), multiply by the ceiling height, and subtract doors and windows. A 12 by 14 foot room with 8 foot ceilings has a perimeter of 52 feet, so 416 square feet of wall before subtracting a door (about 20 square feet) and two windows (about 15 square feet each).

Square footage in real estate

Listings usually quote finished living area, which counts heated, finished space and leaves out garages, unfinished basements and porches. Standards for how to measure differ by country and even by agent, so two sources can give different totals for the same house. When the number matters, such as for a price per square foot, ask what was included and measure it yourself or hire a surveyor.

Price per square foot

Divide a price by the area to compare options. A $2,400 floor that covers 300 square feet costs $8 per square foot. The same method works for rent and for home sales. Use the same unit for both numbers: a price per square meter is about 10.76 times a price per square foot.

Common mistakes

Mixing feet and inches without converting (a room that is 10 feet 6 inches is 10.5 feet, not 10.6). Measuring the room as a single rectangle when it has an alcove. Forgetting closets that need flooring. Forgetting to add waste. And ordering the exact area with no spare, which leaves you short if one piece is damaged. The room list in this calculator lets you add each part separately so none of them is missed.

Worked square footage examples

Example 1: a bedroom and the flooring to buy

A bedroom is 12 feet by 14 feet. Area is 12 × 14 = 168 square feet. With 10% waste you need 168 × 1.10 = 184.8, so buy about 185 square feet. If the planks come in boxes of 20 square feet, that is 10 boxes (9.24 rounds up). At $3.50 per square foot the material costs about $647.50 for the 185 square feet, or $700.00 if you buy all 10 boxes.

Example 2: an L-shaped living room

The main part is 16 by 12 feet, which is 192 square feet. The extra alcove is 8 by 6 feet, which is 48 square feet. The total is 240 square feet. Enter them as two parts in the room list.

Example 3: a round patio

A patio has a radius of 5 feet. Area is 3.1416 × 5 × 5 = 78.54 square feet. If you only know the diameter, halve it first.

Example 4: a room measured in meters

A room is 4.2 by 3.6 meters, so the area is 15.12 square meters. Multiplied by 10.7639, that is about 162.75 square feet. This is useful when a flooring box is labelled in one unit and your tape measure uses the other.

Example 5: a whole apartment

Living room 240, bedroom 168, second bedroom 120, kitchen 90, bathroom 45 and hallway 36 square feet add up to 699 square feet. The calculator adds the rooms for you and shows the total in square feet and square meters, which is helpful for comparing listings in different countries.

Floors, walls, ceilings and outdoor areas

Floor area is length times width. Ceiling area is usually the same as the floor. Wall area is the perimeter times the height, less openings. For a lawn or garden, split it into rectangles, circles and triangles, then add them. Large outdoor areas are often quoted in acres (43,560 square feet) or in hectares (10,000 square meters), so convert before you compare prices. Sod and gravel suppliers usually sell by area or volume, so confirm the unit before you order.

Tile and flooring layout tips

  • Start from the centre of the room so cut pieces at the walls are about the same size.
  • Buy all the material from the same batch, because colour can vary between production runs.
  • Keep a box or two after the job for future repairs.
  • Acclimate wood and laminate in the room for the time the manufacturer recommends before you lay it.
  • Check whether stairs, closets and the area under appliances need covering. They are easy to forget.

Renting and buying by the square foot

Rent and sale prices are often compared per square foot or per square meter. To compare fairly, ask whether the area is carpet area, built-up area or gross area, because the same flat can have different numbers depending on the definition. In India, for example, carpet area, built-up area and super built-up area are three different figures, and the usable floor space is the carpet area. In the US, listings usually give gross living area. Use this calculator to measure the space you can actually use.

Square footage in practice: projects and costs

Flooring cost example

A 200 square foot room with 10% waste needs 220 square feet. With laminate at $2.50 per square foot the material is $550. Add underlay at $0.50 per square foot ($110), and installation at $3 per square foot on the room area ($600), and the project is about $1,260. Prices change a lot by region and product, so use your own quotes, and enter the unit price in the calculator to see the total.

Paint example

A room with 52 feet of wall and 8 foot ceilings has 416 square feet of wall. Subtract one door and two windows (about 50 square feet) to get 366 square feet. Two coats need 732 square feet of coverage, which is about 2 gallons at 400 square feet per gallon. Buy a little extra for touch-ups.

Carpet example

Carpet is sold in 12-foot wide rolls in the US. A 12 by 14 foot room fits on a single 12-foot width, 14 feet long, which is 168 square feet, or 18.7 square yards. A room that is 13 feet wide needs a seam or a second width, so the amount to buy is greater than the floor area.

Heating, cooling and cleaning

Area is also the starting point for sizing heating and cooling, and for planning cleaning supplies. A common rule of thumb for room air conditioners is about 20 BTU per square foot, adjusted for ceiling height, sun and the number of people. Treat this as a first estimate and follow the manufacturer's sizing chart, or ask an installer, before you buy.

Accuracy tips

  • Round measurements up to the nearest inch, not down.
  • Measure in one unit only. Convert feet and inches to decimal feet first (6 inches is 0.5 feet, 3 inches is 0.25 feet).
  • Sketch the room and label each side before you start, so you do not mix up the numbers.
  • For walls and floors that are out of square, measure the longest side.
  • Save your result with Share link or CSV, so you can show the supplier your measurements.

Square footage questions

How do I calculate square footage of a room?

Multiply the length by the width, both in feet. A room 12 feet long and 10 feet wide is 120 square feet. Convert inches to feet first by dividing by 12, so 10 feet 6 inches is 10.5 feet.

How do I calculate square footage of an L-shaped room?

Measure the full length and width as if the room were a rectangle, then subtract the missing corner. A 20 by 15 foot room with an 8 by 6 foot corner missing is 300 − 48 = 252 square feet. You can also split it into two rectangles and add them.

How many square feet are in a square yard?

There are 9 square feet in one square yard, because a yard is 3 feet and 3 × 3 = 9. To convert square feet to square yards, divide by 9. Carpet is often priced per square yard.

How do I convert square feet to square meters?

Divide square feet by 10.764. So 200 square feet is about 18.58 square meters. To go the other way, multiply square meters by 10.764.

How much extra flooring should I buy?

Buy about 10% extra for laminate, vinyl plank, hardwood and carpet, 15% for tile or a diagonal pattern, and 20% for herringbone or rooms with many angles. The extra covers cuts, mistakes and future repairs.

How do I measure square footage of a house?

Measure each room and hallway and add them together, or measure the outside walls and subtract garages, porches and unfinished spaces. Real estate listings usually count finished, heated living space only, so your total may differ from a listing.

How many square feet is a 12 by 12 room?

A 12 by 12 foot room is 144 square feet. With 10% extra for waste, buy about 159 square feet of flooring.
