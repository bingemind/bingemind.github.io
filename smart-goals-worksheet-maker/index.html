What is a SMART goals worksheet?

A SMART goals worksheet is one page that turns a wish into a plan. "Get better at math" is a wish. "Raise my unit test score from 72 to 85 percent in ten weeks" is a goal you can check. The worksheet asks five questions, one for each letter of SMART, and gives you space for steps and check-ins. This SMART goals worksheet maker lets you type the answers, checks each letter, and prints a clean sheet.

How to use the SMART goals worksheet maker

  1. Pick an example, or start blank.
  2. Type your goal in a few words.
  3. Answer the five letters. Each one shows "Done", "Needs work" or "Check this".
  4. Choose a deadline. The check-in table fills with real dates.
  5. Press "Write the goal sentence from my answers", then change the sentence if you want.
  6. Add up to five action steps.
  7. Download the PDF or print it. For a class, use "Download blank PDF".

What the five letters mean

  • Specific. Say exactly what you will do. Name the action, not the feeling. "Walk for 30 minutes" is specific. "Be healthier" is not.
  • Measurable. Add something you can count: a score, a number of times, an amount of money, a number of pages. If you cannot count it, you cannot tell if you are on track.
  • Achievable. Say what makes the goal possible: the time you have, the skills you have, or the help you can get. A goal that needs ten free hours a week will fail if you have two.
  • Relevant. Say why the goal matters to you, your class or your team. A reason helps on the weeks when progress is slow.
  • Time-bound. Pick a deadline. A date turns "someday" into a plan and lets you set check-ins.

How the letter checks work

The checks are simple rules, not a judge of your goal. The Specific check looks for at least four words and flags vague words such as "better" or "more" when there is no number. The Measurable check looks for a number or a number word. The Achievable and Relevant checks only make sure you wrote something. The Time-bound check looks at your dates: the deadline must come after the start, must not be in the past, and should not be more than two years away. A "Done" mark means the answer has the right parts. Only you can decide if the goal is right for you.

SMART goal examples

The examples in the tool are starting points. Change them to fit your own life.

  • Student: "By March 6, I will study algebra for 30 minutes after school on weekdays. How I will measure it: my unit test score, from 72 to 85 percent."
  • Teacher: "I will return every essay with written feedback within 5 school days, tracked in my planner."
  • Nursing student: "I will practice dosage calculation questions 4 times a week until I score 90 percent on two practice quizzes in a row."
  • Work: "I will lead the weekly team meeting for 8 weeks, with the agenda sent one day before."
  • Fitness: "I will walk for 30 minutes on 5 days each week for 8 weeks."
  • Money: "I will move $25 to savings every Friday until I have $500."

Tips for a goal you will keep

  • Write one goal per sheet. Two goals on one page usually means two half plans.
  • Make the first step small enough to do this week.
  • Put the check-in dates in your calendar on the day you print the sheet.
  • At each check-in, write one line of progress and one next step. Change the plan if it is not working. Changing the steps is not failing.
  • If the deadline is far away, set a smaller goal for the first month.

Frequently asked questions

Is this SMART goals worksheet free?

Yes. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no limit. You can download or print as many worksheets as you need.

Does the tool write my goal for me?

No. You write the answers. The page checks each letter with simple rules and can join your answers into one goal sentence. It does not write your goal for you and it does not invent goals.

What does SMART stand for?

Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. Some teachers use "Attainable" or "Realistic" for the A and R. The idea is the same.

Can I print a blank SMART goals worksheet?

Yes. Press "Download blank PDF" or "Print blank". Your typed answers stay in the form.

Can teachers use it with a class?

Yes. Students do not need an account. Print the blank sheet for the class, or fill one example and show it on the board.
