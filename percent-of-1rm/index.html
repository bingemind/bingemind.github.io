What is a percent of 1RM calculator?

A percent of 1RM calculator turns your one rep max into the weight for any percent of it. Many strength programs write loads this way: "3 sets of 5 at 80 percent." You need the number in pounds or kilograms, and you need to know which plates to load. This page does both. Type your 1RM at the top and the full chart appears at once, from 100 percent down to 50 percent.

How to use it

  1. Pick pounds or kilograms.
  2. Enter your 1RM, or tap a common value.
  3. Choose your bar and the smallest plate your gym has.
  4. Read the Load column. It shows the nearest weight the bar can make, and the plates for each side.
  5. Use "Your own percent" for a number that is not in the chart, such as 82.5 percent.

To go the other way, type a weight in the "Find the percent of a weight" box. The page tells you what share of your 1RM that weight is.

The formula

The math is one step: weight = 1RM x percent / 100. For a 225 lb max, 85 percent is 225 x 85 / 100 = 191.25 lb. Nobody can load 191.25 lb. A bar changes in jumps of twice the smallest plate, since you add a plate to each side. With 2.5 lb plates, the jump is 5 lb. The calculator rounds to the nearest load the bar can make above the bar weight, so 191.25 lb becomes 190 lb. The Exact column keeps the true value so you can see how far the rounding moved it.

Reps and percent of 1RM

Lighter weights allow more reps. Researchers have published formulas that link the two. Two common ones are Epley (1985) and Brzycki (1993):

  • Epley: 1RM = weight x (1 + reps / 30). For 1 rep, this page uses the weight itself.
  • Brzycki: 1RM = weight x 36 / (37 - reps)

Turn either one around and you get the weight for a given number of reps. For a 225 lb max:

What percent of 1RM for hypertrophy

For muscle size, most people train sets of about 8 to 12 reps. In the rep table above, that range falls between about 70 and 80 percent of 1RM. For a 225 lb max, 10 reps comes out at 75 percent by both formulas. Pick a percent in this band, then stop each set a rep or two before you fail. What percent of 1RM for 5x5

A 5x5 plan means 5 sets of 5 reps with the same weight. Five reps is worth about 86 percent of your 1RM by Epley and about 89 percent by Brzycki. Five hard sets in a row are a lot more work than one, so many lifters start 5x5 a little lighter, around 80 to 85 percent, and add weight when all 25 reps are clean. Common percents at a glance

Read the table the other way and you can guess how hard a percent is. By both formulas, 90 percent is a set of about 3 to 5 reps, 85 percent is about 5 or 6, 80 percent is about 7 or 8, and 75 percent is about 10. 70 percent is about 12 to 13 reps, and below that the estimates get loose. Treat these as rough guides, since sleep, stress and the lift itself all change what you can do on the day.

One more note on rounding. The tool rounds to the nearest load, so a result can sit a little above or below the exact figure. If you prefer to stay under it, drop to the lighter load in the chart, one jump down. The 100 percent row can sit above your 1RM for the same reason.

Tips

  • If your program uses a training max, enter that number as your 1RM. It is often set below a true max.
  • Plates: tick only the plate sizes your gym has, under "Bar and plates". The worked tables here assume 45, 25, 10, 5 and 2.5 lb plates.
  • An estimated 1RM from a set of reps is less exact than a tested one. Round it down if unsure.
  • If you have an injury, or a heart or blood pressure condition, ask a doctor before you test a max. This page is not medical advice.
  • Use safeties or a spotter on heavy sets.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a percent of my 1RM?

Multiply your 1RM by the percent as a decimal. For a 225 lb 1RM, 85 percent is 225 x 0.85 = 191.25 lb. The calculator does this for every percent from 50 to 100 and then rounds to a weight the bar can load.

What is a 1RM?

1RM means one rep max: the heaviest weight you can lift once with good form. You can test it, or estimate it from a hard set of a few reps. If you only have an estimate, treat every number here as a guide.

Why do the loads not match the exact weight?

A bar only loads in jumps of twice the smallest plate, because plates go on both sides. With 2.5 lb plates the jump is 5 lb. The Exact column shows the true percent. The Load column shows the nearest weight you can build.

How do I find what percent a weight is?

Divide the weight by your 1RM and multiply by 100. If your 1RM is 225 lb, then 185 lb is 185 / 225 = 82.2 percent. Type the weight in the "Find the percent of a weight" box to see it, with a rep estimate.

What do Epley and Brzycki mean?

They are two published formulas that link reps to a max. Epley (1985) says 1RM = weight x (1 + reps / 30). Brzycki (1993) says 1RM = weight x 36 / (37 - reps). The rep table here runs both backwards, to show the weight you might lift for a set number of reps. They are estimates, and they work best for 10 reps or fewer.

Is training at a percent of 1RM safe?

Percent training is a common way to plan loads, but a number is no guarantee. Warm up first, use a spotter or safeties on heavy sets, and lower the weight if your form breaks down. If you have an injury or a heart or blood pressure condition, ask a doctor before you test a max. This page is not medical advice.

What is a training max?

Some programs work from a "training max" set below your true 1RM, often 90 percent of it (the 5/3/1 program by Jim Wendler is one example). If your program says so, enter the training max in the 1RM box instead of your true max.

Does this work in kilograms?

Yes. Switch the unit at the top. Your 1RM converts to the nearest 2.5 kg, and the bar and plate choices change to kilogram sizes. You can also type any kilogram value you like.

Is this a 1 RM calculator?

It is an RM calculator for loads. You enter your 1RM (also written 1 rm or one rm) and get the weight for every percent. The rep table also shows the weight for 1 to 12 reps. It does not estimate your 1RM from a set. The rep formulas here start from a known max.

Can I use it for a bench 1RM or squat 1RM?

Yes. Enter your bench 1RM, squat 1RM or deadlift 1RM in the box. The math is the same for every lift. Pick the bar and plates you use for that lift.
