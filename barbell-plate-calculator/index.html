What a plate calculator does

A plate calculator tells you which weight plates to put on each side of a barbell. You type the total weight you want to lift. The tool subtracts the bar and shows the plates for one side. Load the other side the same way, and the bar stays balanced.

Doing this in your head is easy at 135 lb and slower at 187.5 lb, especially between sets with a pump in your arms. This calculator gives the answer as you type, with a picture of the loaded bar and a short warm-up plan.

How to use the barbell plate calculator

  1. Pick pounds or kilograms.
  2. Enter the total weight you want on the bar, including the bar itself. Use the plus and minus buttons or tap a common weight.
  3. Choose your bar. A standard men's Olympic bar is 45 lb or 20 kg. Many women's bars are 35 lb or 15 kg. Pick Custom for a training bar, a safety squat bar or a trap bar.
  4. Turn off any plate your gym does not have. The calculator then finds the best load from the plates left.
  5. Read the plates for one side, then check the picture. Put the heaviest plates on first, against the bar shoulder. Small plates go on last, then the collar.

The math in one line

Plates per side = (target weight − bar weight) ÷ 2. For 225 lb on a 45 lb bar: (225 − 45) ÷ 2 = 90 lb per side. That is two 45 lb plates. For 100 kg on a 20 kg bar: (100 − 20) ÷ 2 = 40 kg per side. That is one 25 kg plate and one 15 kg plate.

How many plates fit on the bar

A standard sleeve holds about 8 to 10 full-size plates per side, depending on how thick your plates are. The calculator warns you when a load needs more than 10 plates on a side. Use bigger plates, or check that they all sit on the sleeve.

Worked example: a 225 lb work set

Say your top set is 225 lb on a 45 lb bar. The warm-up table at 40, 60 and 80 percent gives you this ramp:

  • 90 lb (40%): two 10 lb plates and one 2.5 lb plate per side.
  • 135 lb (60%): one 45 lb plate per side.
  • 180 lb (80%): one 45 lb, two 10 lb and one 2.5 lb plate per side.
  • 225 lb (work set): two 45 lb plates per side.

The table starts with the empty bar. It rounds each warm-up to 5 lb (2.5 kg in kilogram mode), then to the closest load your plates can make.

What if the exact weight is not possible?

The smallest plate sets the limit. With plates down to 2.5 lb, the smallest step on a 45 lb bar is 5 lb total. A target of 226 lb cannot be built. The calculator shows the closest load, 225 lb, and two cards with the nearest load under and over your target. Tap a card to use it. Switch on the 1.25 lb plate to make the steps 2.5 lb. The 1.25 kg plate does the same in kilograms.

Collars and bar weight

Spring collars weigh about 1 lb each. Competition collars weigh 2.5 kg each. If you want the total to be exact, switch on the collars option and enter the weight of one collar. The calculator subtracts both collars before it works out the plates. Your bar may also differ from the label. Weigh it, or choose Custom and type the real number.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know what plates to put on a barbell?

Subtract the bar weight from your target, then divide by 2. The result is the weight to load on each side. For 185 lb on a 45 lb bar, that is 70 lb per side: one 45 lb plate and one 25 lb plate.

How much does a standard barbell weigh?

A standard Olympic men's bar weighs 45 lb (20 kg). Many women's bars weigh 35 lb (15 kg). Training bars, safety squat bars and trap bars vary, so use the Custom option for those.

Does the target weight include the bar?

Yes. Enter the total weight on the bar, as in "I want to bench 185". The calculator subtracts the bar for you.

What is the smallest plate I can use to make small jumps?

A 1.25 lb or 1.25 kg plate on each side adds 2.5 lb or 2.5 kg to the total. Magnetic micro plates are smaller still. If you keep them on the bar, add their weight to a Custom bar weight.

What if my gym does not have a certain plate?

Pick a plate set above the plate list, or tap a plate to turn it off. The calculator then builds the closest load from the plates that remain.

How do the warm-up sets work?

The table shows the empty bar, your target at 40, 60 and 80 percent (or the percents you type), and the work set. You can change the percents and copy the plan. Each warm-up is rounded to 5 lb or 2.5 kg, then to a weight your plates can make.

Should I count the collars?

Only if you want an exact total. Most lifters skip them for everyday training.

How do I convert between pounds and kilograms?

One kilogram is 2.2046 pounds. Switch the unit at the top of the calculator. Your target converts and rounds to the nearest 5 lb or 2.5 kg step, and the bar and plate choices change to match.
