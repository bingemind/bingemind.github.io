How your daily calories are estimated

The calculator works in two steps.

1. Basal metabolic rate (BMR)

BMR is the energy your body uses at complete rest. We use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which studies have found to be the most accurate of the common formulas for most adults.

Men: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5
Women: BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

2. Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE)

BMR is multiplied by an activity factor to cover movement and exercise:

Activity levelFactor
Sedentary: desk job, little exercise1.2
Light: exercise 1 to 3 days a week1.375
Moderate: exercise 3 to 5 days a week1.55
Active: hard exercise 6 to 7 days a week1.725
Very active: physical job or twice-daily training1.9

Example: a 30-year-old man, 175 cm and 75 kg, has a BMR of 750 + 1,093.75 − 150 + 5 = 1,699 kcal. With moderate activity his TDEE is 1,699 × 1.55 = 2,633 kcal a day.

Calories for losing or gaining weight

Body weight changes when the energy you eat differs from the energy you use. A widely used approximation is that 1 kg of body fat stores about 7,700 kcal, or 3,500 kcal per pound.

  • A deficit of about 500 kcal a day leads to roughly 0.5 kg (1 lb) of loss a week.
  • A deficit of about 1,000 kcal a day leads to roughly 1 kg (2 lb) a week, the fastest rate usually recommended.
  • A surplus of 250 to 500 kcal a day, with strength training, supports gradual weight gain.

Real results are slower than the arithmetic suggests, because your body uses less energy as you get lighter. Recalculate every few kilograms.

Safe minimums

The calculator never shows a target below 1,200 kcal a day for women or 1,500 for men. Very low intakes make it hard to get enough nutrients and should only be followed with medical supervision.

Protein, fat and carbohydrates

The result suggests a simple split:

  • Protein: 1.6 g per kg of body weight. This is at the higher end of what studies show helps keep muscle while losing weight or training. The basic adult requirement is 0.8 g per kg.
  • Fat: 30% of energy, within the 20% to 35% range in most national guidelines.
  • Carbohydrate: the remaining energy.

Protein and carbohydrate provide about 4 kcal per gram, fat 9 kcal, and alcohol 7 kcal.

Calories and kilojoules

Food labels in Australia and New Zealand use kilojoules, and EU and UK labels show both. One kilocalorie is 4.184 kJ. Choose the unit you want under "Show energy in". It is set to kJ automatically for Australia and New Zealand.

Making the number work in real life

  • Treat it as a starting estimate. Formulas can be off by 10% for an individual. Track your weight for two to three weeks and adjust by 100 to 200 kcal.
  • Be honest about activity. Most people with desk jobs who exercise a few times a week are "light" or "moderate", not "active".
  • Food quality still matters. Vegetables, fruit, whole grains, pulses and lean protein make a calorie target far easier to keep than the same energy from snacks.
  • Sleep and steps change how hungry you feel and how much you move without noticing.

This calculator is for adults. Pregnant or breastfeeding women, teenagers, people with eating disorders and anyone with a medical condition should get personal advice. Check where your weight sits with the BMI calculator.

Calorie questions

How many calories should I eat a day?

It depends on your size, age, sex and activity. As a rough guide, many adult women maintain weight on about 2,000 kcal a day and men on about 2,500. Enter your details for a personal estimate.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

About 500 kcal a day below your maintenance level gives a loss of roughly 0.5 kg or 1 lb a week. Avoid going below 1,200 kcal for women or 1,500 for men without medical advice.

What is BMR?

Basal metabolic rate is the energy your body uses at rest to keep you alive: breathing, circulation and cell repair. It is usually 60% to 70% of the calories you burn in a day.

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is energy used at complete rest. TDEE, total daily energy expenditure, adds everything else: movement, exercise and digesting food. TDEE is the number to eat at to keep your weight steady.

Which calorie formula is most accurate?

For most adults, the Mifflin-St Jeor equation predicts resting energy use better than the older Harris-Benedict equation, which is why it is used here.

How do I convert calories to kilojoules?

Multiply kilocalories by 4.184. So 2,000 kcal is 8,368 kJ.

How much protein do I need?

The basic adult requirement is about 0.8 g per kg of body weight a day. People who are losing weight or training often aim for 1.2 to 2 g per kg.
